A Compliment

Image
Text

Maria lived with her mother in a small apartment in New York and worked as a secretary for a large company. Her life was quiet, ordinary, and rather dull. At work, hardly anyone noticed her.

One morning, on her way to the office, Maria saw a newly opened hat shop and went inside out of curiosity. A woman and her young daughter were there, along with another customer looking for a hat.

Maria tried on several hats. One appealed to her more than all the others. The moment she put it on, the little girl exclaimed with delight:

“Mom, look how beautiful that woman looks in the hat!”

The girl’s mother turned to Maria and said:

“Madam, that hat suits you very well.”

The other customer joined them:

“It really does. You look wonderful in it!”

Maria stepped in front of the mirror. For the first time in her life, she liked her reflection so much. Smiling, she went to the counter and paid for the hat.

When Maria left the shop, an entirely new world seemed to open before her. The flowers looked brighter than ever, and the fresh air smelled especially sweet. Even the sounds of traffic and passing voices seemed to form a harmonious melody. Maria felt beautiful and happy.

At the office entrance, the security guard opened the door and wished her good morning. In the elevator, someone asked which floor she needed and pressed the button for her. Throughout the day, her colleagues admired her and told her how lovely she looked, as though they were seeing her for the first time. Her manager even invited her to lunch to ask for her opinion on a new project.

When the magical workday ended, Maria was still smiling. She thought about how suddenly her life had changed and was certain that she owed it all to her new hat.

Maria took a taxi home. Her mother opened the door, stared at her in amazement, and said:

“Maria, you look wonderful! Your eyes are shining just as they did when you were a child!”

“Yes, Mom, it is all because of my new hat. I have had an amazing day!”

“What hat?” her mother asked, puzzled.

Alarmed, Maria touched her head. The hat was gone. She wondered whether she had left it in the taxi, at lunch, or in the office. Then she replayed the scene in the shop: putting on the hat, hearing the compliments, walking to the counter, taking out her purse, and setting the hat down beside the register…

Suddenly she understood. She had forgotten the hat in the shop and walked out without it, her eyes still shining with joy.

The hat had not made Maria happy. It was the confidence and delight awakened by other people’s kind words. The world had not changed; Maria’s way of seeing it had.

Moral: A single kind word can change how a person sees themselves—and sometimes how they see the entire world. Happiness is not found in outward possessions, but within us and in the sincere attention we offer one another.

A Glass of Milk

Image
Text

A poor boy who sold small goods from door to door to pay his tuition discovered one day that he had no money left and was desperately hungry. He decided to knock on the next door and ask for something to eat.

A young woman answered. Embarrassed, the boy could not bring himself to ask for food and requested only a glass of water. Seeing that he was hungry, the woman brought him a large glass of milk instead.

After drinking it slowly, the boy asked:

“How much do I owe you?”

“Nothing,” the woman replied. “My mother taught us to do kindness without expecting anything in return.”

The boy could say only:

“Thank you from the bottom of my heart.”

As he walked away, he felt stronger not only physically, but also in spirit. His faith in God and in other people had been renewed.

Years passed. One day, the same woman became seriously ill. Unable to determine the cause of her illness, the local doctors sent her to specialists in a large city.

Dr. Howard Kelly was called in for a consultation. When he heard which town the patient came from, he immediately went to her room. The moment he saw her, he recognized the woman who had given him a glass of milk years before and rekindled hope in his heart.

Dr. Kelly personally took charge of her treatment and resolved to do everything he could to save her. After a long struggle, the illness was overcome and the woman recovered.

When the time came for her discharge, Dr. Kelly requested the final bill. He wrote a few words along its edge and had it delivered to the patient’s room.

The woman opened it anxiously. She feared that she would never be able to pay the amount and would spend the rest of her life repaying the debt. Then she noticed a sentence written in the margin:

“Paid in full with one glass of milk.”

Signed: Dr. Howard Kelly.

Moral: A selfless act of kindness is never lost. It is entered into an unseen account and may return to us one day—often when we least expect it.

A Handful of Roasted Chickpeas

Image
Text

A boy entered a nut shop with his mother and kept insisting, “I want some roasted chickpeas.” The shopkeeper saw that the mother did not intend to buy any and felt sorry for the child:

“Come here, son. Take a handful for yourself.”

“No, I will take them only if you give them to me,” the boy replied.

The shopkeeper handed him a generous handful and asked:

“Why did you not take them yourself? Why did you want me to give them to you?”

The boy smiled:

“Because your hand is bigger than mine.”

“What a clever child you are!” the shopkeeper said with a laugh.

Moral: Sometimes who you ask matters more than what you ask for. When the giver’s hand is larger, the gift may be larger too.

A Pair of Boots

Image
Text

One day, a schoolteacher asked her pupils to write a story on any subject and promised a pair of boots to the author of the best one. Every child completed a story and handed it in.

The teacher read each piece and liked them all. Unable to choose the best, she decided to draw the winner by lot.

Each pupil wrote a name on a slip of paper, folded it, and placed it in a box on the teacher’s desk. After mixing the slips, she drew one and announced:

“Amina!”

The classroom erupted in applause. Amina pressed the boots to her chest and could not hold back tears of joy.

That evening, the teacher returned home and tearfully told her husband what had happened. He was puzzled:

“You found a wonderful solution. Why are you crying?”

The teacher replied:

“Because not one slip bore the writer’s own name. Every child had written Amina—the pupil from the poorest family in the class. They all wanted her to receive the boots.”

Moral: The finest victory is to value another person’s joy above one’s own. True compassion is revealed in the willingness to surrender an opportunity for someone else.

Discussion and Conflict

Image
Text

As every day, Javid Bey and his team gathered in the meeting room to discuss the status of the project they were working on. Everyone briefly reported the successful results of the previous day as well as the difficulties they had encountered.

It was Kamal’s turn. Like the others, he reported on the work he had done, but — as if hoping for help from his colleagues — he spoke in more detail about the problem he faced. As soon as he finished, Nadir accused him in front of everyone: “You are to blame; because of you our work has stopped and we can do nothing.” The room froze. Kamal was deeply embarrassed and did not know what to do.

As project manager, Javid Bey immediately intervened so the talk would not turn into a conflict, and said to the team: “Friends, if you like, let’s end the discussion for today and settle this matter another time.” Everyone dispersed.

Javid Bey knew exactly what to do: meet one-to-one and talk as friends. He invited Nadir to the company café under the pretext of a cup of tea; after some small talk he finally came to the point and said calmly, without losing composure: “My son, your behaviour toward Kamal today was very wrong. It is unacceptable to accuse someone without knowing the truth of the matter — and in front of others. Put yourself in his place. He expected help from us and was met with blame. I am sure Kamal hoped we would sit together, think it through, and find a solution as a team.”

Nadir lowered his head in shame. Javid Bey’s counsel had worked; it was a good lesson for Nadir — he understood he had done wrong.

Later, whenever the moment arose, he would recall that conversation and say he was always grateful to Javid Bey. Importantly, no one else in the company ever learned of that talk.

Moral: Conflict asks who is right; discussion asks what is right. If you want the truth, discuss — do not quarrel.

Everyone Is the Same Here

Image
Text

A newly appointed young head of the district municipality decided to walk around and learn the situation. After walking the area alone, he entered a teahouse on a side street and ordered tea.

Just then a twelve- or thirteen-year-old child said:

— Uncle, shall I polish your shoes?

Though his shoes were already polished, not wishing to hurt the child’s heart, he said:

— All right, come polish them. If you do it well, I’ll give you twice what you ask. — Then the child replied:

— I polish them the same way for everyone.

The official said:

— What do you mean?

The child answered:

— Our teacher told us: “Children, whatever you do, treat everyone the same — do not discriminate.” With this money I will buy medicine for my sick mother. If I serve you differently, that medicine will not heal her!

The young official had learned one of the most important lessons of his life. He was deeply moved and gave the child the largest note in his pocket, and his card as well. He promised to look after this fatherless boy who both studied and cared for his sick mother.

He also went to see the teacher who had instilled that honesty in the child, and thanked him separately.

So as not to forget what he had heard from the shoe-shine boy, and to apply it always in his work, he had the sentence “Everyone is the same here” written on the back of the nameplate on his desk.

Though it was hard, he always tried to put it into practice.

Moral: Treat a person the same because they are human, not because of their rank. Society is changed by those who choose what is right in small moments.

Text

Forgiving someone does not mean loving them; it does not mean having to talk to them, continuing the relationship; it does not mean accepting that they are innocent, right.

Forgiving someone means getting out of the prison of disappointment, resentment, hatred, and no longer feeling the pain of the wound inflicted on you.

Forgive and be free. Forgive, but never forget, otherwise you will be hurt again.

Moral: Forgiving is not about justifying the other person - it is about freeing yourself from the prison of hatred and resentment. Forgive and be relieved, but do not forget the lesson.

Gossip

Image
Text

A bitter, impolite man was summoned to the village elder, accused of spreading gossip.

— It was a joke, just a joke, — he denied the accusation. — Others misunderstood my words, I am not guilty!

But the victim of the gossip stood firm:

— You have tarnished my clean name. I demand justice.

The culprit retreated as he was pressed:

— I take back everything I said, I ask for forgiveness.

The elder listened silently. It crossed his mind: “This man has not yet realized the gravity of what he has done. Time will pass, and he will repeat the same sin again.” Then he said to the man:

— Go and bring a feather pillow to the market square. Cut it, scatter the feathers around. Then collect all the feathers one by one and bring them to me. If you cannot do this, there is no forgiveness.

The man reluctantly accepted the elder's condition. “The old man has lost his mind,” he thought, and then he formed and cut the pillow. The feathers scattered in a single blow and flew in all directions. The man ran to the right and left, chasing the feathers; but no matter how hard he tried, in the end only a few feathers remained in his palm.

He lowered his head and returned to the elder:

— I could not collect the rest.

Then he sighed deeply:

— Just like the words I spread. I cannot take them back.

Moral: A word that is spread is like a feather thrown to the wind - it cannot be collected again. Therefore, stay away from gossip.

How to overcome insults

Image
Text

(An instructive story from the life of the samurai)

There was a wise and wise samurai. He taught his students the secrets of wisdom and martial arts. One day, during the next lesson, a warrior known for his rudeness and cruelty entered the class. This warrior would insult his opponents and get them angry, and then, taking advantage of the mistakes made by his angry opponent one after another, he would defeat him. This time, he used the same tactic, saying a few insults to the samurai to provoke him into a fight. The samurai ignored the insults and continued his lesson.

After repeating his insults several times, the warrior, who could not get any response from the samurai, left the class in a state of anger.

The students, who were watching this scene carefully, asked their teacher:

— Master, why did you tolerate these insults? Why didn’t you challenge him to a fight?

The wise samurai answers:

— If someone brings you a gift and you do not accept it, then to whom does the gift belong?

— To the previous owner, — the disciples answer.

— Envy, hatred, and insults should be viewed in the same way. If you do not accept them, such “gifts” remain with those who tried to address them to you.

Moral: An insult that you do not accept returns to its owner — just like a gift that is returned. Do not return evil for evil, but with moderation.

Humanity

Image
Text

He studied for years, worked hard, and finally the day of the exam that would determine his fate arrived... His father, Kazim, was a rather demanding man, always closely interested in his son's studies and thought that he would definitely succeed in this exam...

Kamal entered the building and found the class where he would take the exam. Although he was excited when the question and answer sheets were distributed, he was very relieved when the exam began... He answered the questions easily, and he was completely sure of the correctness of the answers... There was half an hour left before the exam ended. He was so confident in himself that...

Suddenly, he fixed his eyes intently on the classroom window. Without thinking about what he saw, he got up and ran towards the door...

— Go back! Otherwise, your exam will be canceled, a year will be wasted, my son, — the teacher who was taking the exam called out to him. But he did not stop, he ran out the back door of the building...

His father, who came home a few hours later, first scolded Kamal, and then even slapped him in the face. Kazim was very upset. Even though he asked why he had left the class and why he had left the exam unfinished, Kamal kept quiet because he knew that his father would be even more angry... If he had said so, he would not have understood...

Kazim scolded and reprimanded his son all that year. Although Kamal was hurt by this situation, he did not listen, he kept his head down and focused on his studies.

The following year, he took the exam again and was accepted into the medical faculty... He became a very good doctor. Despite living in the city for many years, he asked for a voluntary assignment to a remote village hospital. After working there for a year, his family came to visit him at his house... The exam issue between him and his father had long been forgotten...

On a snowy and cold evening, Kamal's home phone rang. The caller said that there was a patient in one of the nearby villages. When he picked up his bag and set off, his father Kazim did not want to leave his son alone on the mountain roads, so he decided to accompany him. They walked for about an hour under a heavy blizzard and reached that village. They found the house and knocked on the door. An old man opened the door, said that his wife was sick, and invited the doctor in with his father...

Kamal examined the patient and prescribed the necessary treatment. The old woman thanked the doctor and said in a trembling voice: "You have helped us in this cold, my son. What would this world be like if there were no good people like you? Being a human is not easy... Listen."

"Years ago, I went to the city. Suddenly, in the middle of the road, I felt bad, I was in severe pain. There was no one around, the roads were empty. By the way, there was an exam that day. Less than five minutes later, a young man approached me in excitement. He took me to the hospital in half an hour in that heat. I was pregnant, and I was trembling with fear that something would happen to my child. The doctor in the admissions department said that if I had been ten minutes late, I could have died... That day my son was born.

I learned the name of the boy who brought me to the hospital - he said it was Kamal. He said, "I was in the exam, I saw you when I looked out the window, and when I realized that you were alone, I left the classroom and came to your aid. Even though I lost a year because I gave up the exam, I did not regret it at all.

I named my son Kamal. I raised him with the story of that kind boy, I always advised him to be a person like Kamal. Now he is a teacher... He tries to raise his students as human beings first and foremost... Oh, I wish I could find that boy and get halal from him... Anyone can be a doctor, teacher, engineer. The most difficult thing in this world is to be a human being... What a wonderful person that young man was. He did not leave me alone in the middle of the road." Hearing this, Dr. Kamal said: "It is halal, mother, don't worry...".

Although his father Kazim was a handsome man, he did not want to show his tears. However, when he left the house, tears wet his cheeks. Even after he set off, Kazim's tears did not stop. He said in a trembling voice, "Kamal, my son," but he couldn't continue because he was sobbing.

Moral: It's possible to have a profession, but the real challenge is to be a human being. A kindness done in a young person's time becomes a blessing that lasts for generations.

I was ashamed, by God

Image
Text

It was during the COVID-19 pandemic. Your sword, as they say, was cutting both the branch and the front. After a long break, the following conversation takes place between two people who meet:

— Hello, seagull, what's up? It's been a long time since you've seen each other. Where have you been?

— No! I had COVID.

— What? What is COVID?

— What do you mean, what is it? You don't know what COVID is?

— By God, I have no idea.

— So you don't know. So you're saying you've never had COVID?

— No, by God, I've never had it.

— Shame on you. But don't you know that in this day and age, it's considered a shame not to have COVID?

— No, by God, that's not possible, are you right?

— May there never be a dawn!

— Thank you very much for opening my eyes. Then let me go and get COVID too, or I'll be a disgrace to the community. I was ashamed, by God.

Moral: It is ridiculous to follow everything the crowd says is "shameful." Wisdom lies not in the gossip of the crowd, but in one's own thoughts.

If the road is not tiring, it is because of your husband

Image
Text

My mother used to say: "I married your father when I was very young. I could neither cook a proper meal nor pick up a needle and embroider. One day, when my mother-in-law—your grandmother—was getting ready to go to the neighbor's house, she spread out a blanket in front of me that she had washed and dried, and said, 'You can finish sewing this before I get back.' I started sewing, but I couldn't do it. The needle wouldn't go through the blanket. I started to sew. On the one hand, the needle was digging into my hand, and my eyes were watering from the pain, and on the other hand, my tears wouldn't stop because I didn't know what to say to my mother-in-law.

At that time, my father came home for lunch. Seeing me crying, he picked up the needle, finished sewing the blanket in a short time, and put it aside. Then he ate his meal and went to work. He didn't say anything to my mother-in-law. When the woman returned home, she saw that the blanket was completely ready and was very pleased."

It's not for nothing that they say: "If the road doesn't tire you, it's because of your companion."

Moral: It's not the road that tires you or lightens your load, but the person next to you. A true companion makes even the hardest task easier.

Mercy

Image
Text

It was a cold winter night. I was returning home from work. I saw a man in front of me who looked like a drunk. He was leaning back and forth, weakly clinging to a pole next to him. I approached him. He was over thirty. He was very clean and neatly dressed. Some of the passers-by were talking loudly about the dangers of alcohol, while others were laughing mockingly.

— Are you okay, do you need anything? — I asked the man.

In a voice that sounded like a groan coming from his lips:

— I am sick!… — he said.

I held him by the waist with my arm so that he wouldn’t fall and led him to the taxi stand. We started waiting for a taxi. The snow that had stopped in the evening was falling again; there were no signs of life except for stray dogs racing each other on the slowly icy roads.

I had given up hope of finding a taxi when a taxi stopped next to us. I explained the situation to the driver and told him that we were in a hurry.

We put the patient in the back seat and headed to the hospital. Thankfully, the doctors did everything they could right away, administered medication; we waited at the man's bedside.

The doctor on duty said that we had at least saved the patient from freezing. The young man, who could not speak yet, just looked at us and smiled. The driver was with me... Our patient came to his senses after a while. We put him back in the car and took him home.

His wife, knowing that he often fell into diabetic comas, had gone out into the street with her son in a terrible panic. When they saw us, they ran to us excitedly. The family members hugged each other tightly, their eyes full of joy.

There was no trace of our fatigue that had lasted for hours; our eyes were filled with the happiness of that family that did not know how to thank us.

When we said goodbye, I asked the driver what I owed him.

He shook his head:

— You are not in debt, on the contrary, you have a debt, my friend! — he said. — You have made me a partner in such a good thing. I am indebted to you for giving this wonderful feeling to a person who has probably forgotten what it is to cry for twenty years.

I did not feel the cold of the night as I hugged the driver and left, and decided to walk home.

Who knows? Maybe another person would come across me on my way who needed my help?

Moral: Humanity is built on compassion. Compassion is not crying with the crying person, but finding a way to stop the tears.

Permission or Apology

Image
Text

There is a saying attributed to Rear Admiral Grace Hopper that says, “It’s better to ask for permission than to ask for it.”

This saying is used in the sense of “Ask for permission first to do something — do it, and then apologize if you’re not happy.”

Sometimes waiting for permission is a missed opportunity. Do what you know is right, and apologize later if necessary.

Imagine that a company has a clear problem that is slowing down the work process. For example, every week the same information is manually transferred to several different spreadsheets, which takes hours. An employee realizes that this can be solved with simple automation.

If he first tries to get permission at length, the issue can be passed from one manager to another, waiting for weeks for a response, and the opportunity can be lost. However, the employee, realizing that the risk is small, sets up a separate test system without damaging the existing data, automates the process, and shows the result. If the system is successful, everyone benefits from it. If someone is unhappy that a procedure was not agreed upon in advance, the employee can say, “I was just trying to speed things up. If I broke a rule, I apologize.”

Moral: The point here is not to “ignore the rules.” The point is that in situations where the outcome is reversible, the risk is low, and the benefit is clear, expecting too much permission can kill the initiative. Sometimes it is better to act responsibly and show results than to expect endless approval.

Why do people shout when they argue?

Image
Text

One day during class, a teacher asked his students:

— Why do people shout when they argue?

— Because they can’t control their temper, — one student answered.

— After all, the person you’re arguing with is right next to you, so why shout? Can’t you just talk to him calmly? If you’re angry, why should you shout? — the teacher deepened his question.

Each of the students gave an answer, but none of them satisfied the teacher. Finally, he said:

— When people are unhappy with each other, their hearts move away from each other. They have to shout to hear each other from this distance. The louder they shout, the further their hearts are from each other.

After a moment of silence, the teacher continued:

— But what happens when people love each other? They don’t shout, on the contrary, they speak softly. Because their hearts are very close to each other, the distance between them is small. But what happens when there is stronger love? Then they don't even speak softly, they whisper and get a little closer to each other. As their hearts get closer, they don't even need to whisper — they just look at each other and understand everything without words... My dear students, when you argue, don't let your hearts drift apart, don't say words that could increase the distance between you.

Because one day this distance may grow so much that you won't be able to find your way back.

Moral: Screaming is the sound of hearts drifting apart. A whisper is enough in love — don't increase the distance between you.
