A Compliment

Text

Maria lived with her mother in a small apartment and worked as a secretary at a large company. Her days all seemed much the same. At work, she often felt invisible: she arrived, completed her tasks, and quietly went home.

One morning, she noticed a newly opened hat shop on her way to work. A hat in the window caught her eye, and she stepped inside.

A little girl was there with her mother, while another customer was choosing a hat. Maria tried on several. As she put on the last one, the girl called out excitedly:

“Mom, look! Doesn’t that lady look beautiful in that hat?”

Her mother turned and smiled.

“It really does suit you.”

The other customer nodded.

“As though it were made for you!”

Maria looked in the mirror. It had been a long time since she had looked at herself so closely—or so kindly. She smiled and stood a little straighter.

She decided to buy the hat, went to the counter, paid, and left the shop.

It was the same street, yet everything seemed different. The flowers outside the florist’s looked brighter, and the morning air felt fresher. Even the city’s noise seemed to have a pleasant rhythm.

At the office, she returned the security guard’s greeting with a smile. In the lift, someone pressed the button for her floor, and she thanked them warmly. She spoke more easily with her colleagues and joined their conversations with interest.

“You’re glowing today!” one of them said.

At lunchtime, her manager invited her out to discuss a new project. This time, Maria shared her ideas without holding back.

She was still smiling when she returned home that evening. Thinking over the day, she marveled: “Who would have thought a hat could make such a difference?”

Her mother opened the door and beamed.

“Maria, you look lovely! Your eyes are shining the way they did when you were little.”

“It’s all thanks to my new hat, Mom! I’ve had such a wonderful day.”

Her mother looked puzzled.

“What hat?”

Maria reached up. There was no hat.

For a moment, she was bewildered. Had she left it in the taxi? At the office? Then she retraced the moments in the shop: the mirror, the kind words, the counter… She had taken the hat off and put it down while reaching for her purse.

And there she had left it.

Maria smiled. It had not been the hat that made the difference. A few sincere words had helped her see herself more kindly and let her happiness show.

The world was still the same. But Maria no longer felt invisible in it.

Moral: A sincere compliment can remind someone of a beauty they struggle to see in themselves. Do not hold back a kind word: sometimes a single sentence can give someone’s confidence a much-needed lift.

A Glass of Milk

Audio
Image
Text

A well-known story associated with Dr. Howard Kelly goes like this:

A young man was selling small household items door to door to pay for his education. One day, hungry and out of money, he decided to ask for something to eat at the next house.

A young woman opened the door. Embarrassed, he lost his courage.

“Could I please have a glass of water?”

She noticed his tired face and sensed that he was hungry. A moment later, she returned with a large glass of milk.

He drank it slowly, then asked:

“How much do I owe you?”

“Nothing,” she said with a smile. “My mother taught us to give without expecting anything in return.”

“Then thank you, from the bottom of my heart.”

As he walked away, it was not only his hunger that had eased. He felt less alone, and his faith in God and in human kindness had been renewed.

Years passed. The woman became seriously ill, and her local doctors referred her to a hospital in a larger city.

Dr. Howard Kelly was asked to consult on her treatment. When he heard where she came from, a distant memory stirred. He went to her room and recognized her immediately.

She was the woman who had given him the milk.

Dr. Kelly took a close interest in her care. After a long course of treatment, she recovered.

On the day she was to leave, he asked to see the bill before it was delivered. He wrote a few words on it and had it sent to her room.

She was afraid to open the envelope. How many years would it take her to pay?

At last, she unfolded the bill. Her eyes filled with tears as she read the note:

“Paid in full with a glass of milk.”

Beneath it was the signature:

Dr. Howard Kelly.

Moral: Give because someone needs your kindness, not because you expect it to return. A small act of care can leave a lasting impression and inspire another kindness years later.

A Handful of Roasted Chickpeas

Audio
Image
Text

A little boy went into a nut shop with his mother. As soon as he spotted the roasted chickpeas, he began pleading:

“Mom, can I have some? Please!”

When the shopkeeper realized that his mother wasn’t going to buy any, he decided to treat the boy.

“Come on, help yourself to a handful. They’re on me.”

But the boy kept his hands by his sides.

“I’d rather you gave them to me.”

The shopkeeper scooped up a generous handful and handed them over. Then he asked:

“Why didn’t you take some yourself? Why did you want me to do it?”

The boy grinned.

“Because your hands are bigger than mine!”

The shopkeeper burst out laughing.

“You clever little fellow!”

Moral: Sometimes noticing a small difference can work to your advantage. Being quick-witted means spotting what others overlook.

A Pair of Boots

Audio
Image
Text

One day, a teacher asked her pupils to write a story on any subject they liked. She promised a pair of boots to the writer of the best story.

The children finished their stories and handed them in. The teacher read them one by one and enjoyed them all. Unable to choose a favorite, she decided to draw lots.

She asked each pupil to write their own name on a slip of paper, fold it, and place it in the box on her desk. She mixed the slips, drew one out, unfolded it, and read aloud:

“Amina!”

The classroom burst into applause. Amina hugged the boots to her chest, unable to hold back her tears of joy.

That evening, the teacher’s eyes filled with tears as she told her husband what had happened. Puzzled, he asked:

“What a lovely way to settle it! But why are you crying?”

She replied:

“Afterwards, I opened all the other slips. Not one child had written their own name. Every single one had written Amina’s—the girl whose family had the least. They all wanted her to win the boots.”

Moral: Finding joy in someone else’s happiness is a victory too. Sometimes true compassion means quietly giving up your own chance for someone who needs it more.

Discussion and Conflict

Audio
Image
Text

As they did every day, Javid Bey and his team gathered in the meeting room to discuss the state of their project. Each person briefly described the previous day’s progress and any difficulties they had encountered.

It was Kamal’s turn. Like the others, he reported on the work he had done, but — as if hoping for help from his colleagues — he spoke in more detail about the problem he faced. As soon as he finished, Nadir accused him in front of everyone: “You are to blame; because of you our work has stopped and we can do nothing.” The room froze. Kamal was deeply embarrassed and did not know what to do.

Before the exchange could escalate, Javid Bey, the project manager, stepped in. “Let’s stop here for today and return to this later,” he said. The team dispersed.

Javid Bey knew what to do: speak to Nadir privately and in a friendly way. He invited him to the company café for a cup of tea. After a little small talk, he said calmly, “My son, the way you treated Kamal today was wrong. You accused him without understanding the situation, and you did it in front of everyone. Put yourself in his place. He was hoping for our help and was accused instead. I’m sure he hoped we would think the problem through together and find a solution.”

Nadir lowered his head in shame. Javid Bey’s words had taught him a lesson: he understood that he had acted wrongly.

Later, whenever Nadir remembered the conversation, he thanked Javid Bey. He was also grateful that their conversation had remained private.

Moral: Conflict focuses on who is right; discussion focuses on what is right. If you want the truth, discuss — do not quarrel.

Everyone Is Equal Here

Audio
Image
Text

A newly elected young head of the local council decided to explore the neighborhood on foot. After walking for a while, he stopped at a teahouse on a side street and ordered some tea.

A boy of twelve or thirteen approached him.

“Would you like your shoes polished, sir?”

His shoes were already clean, but he did not want to turn the boy away.

“All right. Do a good job, and I’ll pay you double.”

The boy answered calmly:

“I do a good job for everyone.”

The young man looked surprised.

“What do you mean?”

“Our teacher says we should do our work honestly for everyone. It’s not right to do it carefully for one person and carelessly for another.”

Taking out his brush, he added:

“I buy medicine for my mother with this money. I want to earn it honestly.”

The young man fell silent. The boy’s simple words held an important lesson for his own work: good service should not depend on extra money or someone’s status.

When the boy finished, he paid him for his work and gave him some additional money to help. As they talked, he learned that the boy’s father had died. The boy attended school and worked afterward to help his mother.

The young man gave him his contact number and said he would visit the family to see how he could support them. Later, he also met the boy’s teacher and thanked him for teaching his pupils the value of honesty.

To remember their conversation, he had a sentence written on the side of his desk nameplate that faced him:

“Everyone matters equally here.”

He tried to keep those words in mind whenever he made a decision: whoever stood before him deserved the same respect and fair treatment.

Moral: You should not need a wealthy or influential customer to do your work conscientiously. Honest service is measured by respect for each person’s rights, not by the promise of extra money.

Forgiveness

Text

Forgiving someone does not oblige you to love them, speak to them, or continue the relationship. Nor does it mean accepting that they were innocent or right.

Forgiveness means no longer allowing hurt, resentment, and hatred to rule your life. It means gradually easing the weight of past pain.

Give yourself time. When you are ready to forgive, let go of that burden without forgetting what you have learned. As you make room for peace, protect your boundaries too.

Moral: Forgiveness does not excuse a wrong; it means choosing not to carry its burden forever. You can forgive while recognizing that trusting again and continuing the relationship are separate choices.

Gossip

Audio
Image
Text

A sharp-tongued man had been spreading rumors about someone in his village. When the matter came to a head, he was called before the village elder.

“It was only a joke!” he protested. “People misunderstood me. How is that my fault?”

But the man whose reputation he had damaged would not let the matter rest.

“You have dragged my good name through the mud. I want justice.”

Unable to deny what he had said, the gossip finally backed down.

“All right. I take it all back. Please forgive me.”

The elder listened carefully. Sensing that the man still did not understand the harm he had caused, he said:

“Bring a feather pillow to the marketplace. Cut it open and scatter the feathers on the wind. Then gather every last one and bring them to me. After that, we can talk about forgiveness.”

The man reluctantly agreed. What a strange thing to ask, he thought as he carried the pillow to the square, cut it open, and shook the feathers into the air.

The wind scattered them in every direction. He ran back and forth, trying to catch them, but however hard he tried, he managed to collect only a handful.

He returned to the elder with his head bowed.

“I couldn’t gather the rest. The wind carried them everywhere.”

“Do you understand now how difficult it is to take back your words?” the elder asked.

The man looked at the feathers in his hand and sighed.

“Yes. My words have spread just like these feathers. Saying ‘I take it back’ cannot bring them home.”

Moral: Gossip is like feathers scattered on the wind: easy to spread, hard to gather back. An apology matters, but it cannot always undo the harm. Think before you speak.

Unwelcome gifts

Audio
Image
Text

(A parable about a samurai)

An elderly, wise samurai taught his students the ways of wisdom and the art of combat.

One day, a warrior known for his rudeness and cruelty entered during a lesson. He had a favorite tactic: he would insult his opponents until they lost their temper, then exploit the mistakes they made in anger to defeat them.

This time, he used the same tactic to provoke the samurai into a fight. He hurled insults at him, but the samurai paid no attention and continued teaching.

The warrior repeated his insults several times. When he still received no response, he left in frustration.

The students, who had watched closely, asked:

“Master, why did you put up with those insults? Why didn’t you challenge him to a fight?”

The samurai answered with a question:

“If someone brings you a gift and you do not accept it, who keeps it?”

“The person who brought it,” the students replied.

“Think of envy, hatred, and insults in the same way. If you do not accept these ‘gifts,’ they remain with the person who brought them.”

Moral: Do not accept someone else’s insult as the truth about you. Meet hostility with composure rather than hostility of your own, so that another person’s anger does not dictate your behavior.

Humanity

Audio
Image
Text

Kamal had studied and worked hard for years. At last, the day of the examination that could shape his future had arrived. His father, Kazim, was a demanding man. He followed his son’s studies closely and was certain he would succeed.

Kamal found the examination room and took his seat. He felt nervous when the papers were handed out, but relaxed as he read the questions. He knew the answers and wrote with confidence.

With half an hour left, he looked up and glanced through the window. Suddenly, he sprang from his chair and ran toward the door.

“Come back! If you leave now, your exam will be invalidated! You’ll lose a whole year!” the proctor called.

Kamal did not stop. He raced down the stairs and out through the back of the building.

When he returned home several hours later, his father already knew what had happened. He scolded his son, then lost control of his anger and slapped him.

“Why did you leave? Why did you abandon the exam?”

Kamal remained silent. He did not believe his father would listen to him then. He feared that anything he said would only make him angrier.

Throughout the following year, Kazim kept bringing up the incident. Each time, it hurt Kamal, but he did not argue. He studied again and, the next year, earned a place at medical school.

Years passed. Kamal became a skilled, compassionate doctor. Although he had grown up in the city, he chose to work at a hospital in a remote village. Some time later, his family came to visit. The examination was no longer mentioned.

One snowy, bitterly cold evening, the telephone rang. A woman in a nearby village was ill, and someone was asking for help.

Kamal picked up his medical bag. His father did not want him walking the mountain roads alone in such weather.

“I’m coming with you.”

They walked through the blizzard for almost an hour before finding the house. An elderly man opened the door.

“It’s my wife, Doctor. Please come in.”

Kamal examined the woman, gave her initial treatment, and explained the care she would need.

She looked at him gratefully.

“You came to help us in this terrible cold, my son,” she said in a trembling voice. “What would the world be like without people like you? You remind me of a young man…”

She paused to catch her breath.

“Years ago, I went to the city. I was pregnant. Suddenly, I became unwell in the street and was seized by terrible pains. There was nobody nearby to ask for help. I learned later that an examination was taking place in the building beside me.

“Before long, a young man came running. He went to such trouble to get me to the hospital in that heat. It took us half an hour. The doctor said that any further delay could have put my life in danger. My son was born that very day.”

She fell silent for a moment, then smiled.

“I asked the young man his name. He said it was Kamal. He had seen me through the window during his exam and, realizing I was alone, had run out to help.

“‘I can start my studies a year later,’ he told me. ‘I couldn’t leave you there.’”

Kazim looked up at his son. Kamal was listening without a word.

“I named my son Kamal,” the woman continued. “I brought him up hearing about that young man’s kindness. I wanted him to become just as compassionate. He’s a teacher now. Alongside his lessons, he tries to teach his pupils to care about others.”

Her eyes filled with tears.

“If only I could see that young man again. I would tell him that the year he lost gave two people a future. I would ask him to forgive me for what helping me had cost him. You can learn a profession, my son. What matters is never losing your humanity. He did not leave me alone in that street.”

Kamal gently took her hand.

“You owe me nothing,” he said softly. “Please be at peace.”

Kazim lowered his eyes. Inside the house, he tried to hide his tears. Once outside, he could no longer hold them back.

He wept as he walked beside his son along the snowy road.

“Kamal, my son…”

He could say no more. That night, in a stranger’s home, he had finally heard the answer he had refused to listen to years before.

Moral: Our worth lies not only in our profession, but in the choices we make when someone needs us. What looks like a loss may bring lasting good to many lives. Listen before you judge.

Audio

Text

My mother used to tell me:

“I was very young when I married your father. I could barely cook, and I wasn’t much good at sewing either.

“One day, my mother-in-law—your grandmother—was getting ready to visit a neighbor. She had washed and dried the wool filling for a quilt. She spread the quilt out in front of me and said:

“‘Have this stitched by the time I get back.’

“I picked up the needle and began. But however hard I tried, I struggled to push it through the thick quilt. I kept pricking my fingers. In the end, I started crying. My hand hurt, and I had no idea what I would say to her if I couldn’t finish.

“Just then, your father came home for his lunch break. When he saw me crying, he sat beside me and took the needle from my hand. He finished stitching the quilt himself, ate his lunch, and went back to work.

“He never said a word to his mother. When she returned and saw the finished quilt, she was very pleased.”

Whenever I remember my mother’s story, I think of the saying: “If the road feels easy, thank your companion.”

Moral: Life’s journey is lighter with someone who shares your burdens instead of judging you when you struggle. Sometimes love is shown not through grand declarations, but through a quietly offered helping hand.

Compassion

Audio
Image
Text

I was walking home from work on a bitter winter night when I noticed a man struggling to stay on his feet. He swayed, clinging to a post by the roadside to keep from falling.

He looked about thirty and was neatly dressed. Some passers-by assumed he was drunk and made loud remarks about drinking. Others laughed at him. But no one stopped to ask what was wrong.

I approached him.

“Are you all right? Do you need help?”

His lips barely moved.

“I’m ill…”

I put an arm around his waist to steady him, then slowly helped him to a nearby taxi stand.

Snow had begun to fall again. The street was emptying, and the cold was seeping into our bones. I was almost ready to give up hope of finding a taxi when one pulled up beside us.

I explained the situation to the driver. Together, we helped the man into the back seat and headed for the hospital.

The medical staff attended to him immediately. We waited. The driver stayed too.

After a while, the doctor on duty told us:

“It’s a good thing you didn’t leave him out there. His life was at risk in that cold.”

As the man recovered, he looked at us and managed a faint smile. He was still too weak to speak, but his eyes said a great deal.

When the doctors said he could go home, we helped him back into the same taxi.

As we approached his house, we saw his wife and son outside. Knowing that he had diabetes, his wife had grown anxious when he failed to return. She and their son had come out to wait for him.

The moment the taxi stopped, they ran towards us. When the man stepped out, they wrapped their arms around him. For a while, none of them let go.

The hours of weariness vanished. Seeing their joy brought tears to our eyes too.

After we said goodbye, I asked the driver:

“How much do I owe you?”

He shook his head.

“You don’t owe me anything, my friend. I owe you. You let me be part of something good. I don’t think I’ve cried in twenty years… Tonight, you gave me that feeling again.”

I hugged him. When we parted, the night no longer felt quite so cold. I decided to walk home.

Who knew? Perhaps I would meet someone else along the way who needed help.

Moral: Compassion goes beyond feeling sorry for someone. It begins when we stop, ask, and offer a helping hand. Sometimes approaching a person instead of judging them can change a whole family’s world.

Permission or Forgiveness?

Audio
Image
Text

Rear Admiral Grace Hopper helped popularize the saying:

“It’s easier to ask forgiveness than it is to get permission.”

This is sometimes understood as: act first, then apologize if anyone objects. But there is a difference between encouraging initiative and using the saying to excuse irresponsibility.

Sometimes, waiting endlessly for approval prevents a good idea from ever being tried.

Imagine a company where the same information is copied manually into several spreadsheets every week, taking hours. An employee realizes that a simple automated process could make the task much easier.

As the proposal passes from one manager to another, a response could take weeks. Instead, the employee builds a separate demonstration without altering existing data or disrupting anyone’s work. They show their colleagues the result: a task that once took hours now takes only minutes.

When someone objects that the trial was not agreed on in advance, the employee explains:

“I built a separate demonstration to see how we could make the work easier. I haven’t changed the existing system. If I should have let you know beforehand, I apologize.”

The point is to test a useful idea responsibly, rather than disregard the rules. When the risk is low, mistakes can be corrected, and other people’s rights are respected, a small initiative can demonstrate more than lengthy discussions.

Moral: Initiative becomes valuable when it is paired with responsibility. Showing what is possible through small, reversible steps can be more effective than waiting endlessly for approval. But apologizing afterwards does not remove the responsibility to think beforehand.

“My wife has passed away, and my son lives abroad. I live here alone. Who knows how much time I have left?”

I noticed tears gathering in his eyes.

“But didn’t you subscribe because you wanted to read the newspaper?” I asked.

“I don’t read newspapers, my boy... I subscribed to the sound of the doorbell.”

Then he clasped his hands and said quietly:

“Do me one more kindness. This is my son’s phone number. If you knock one day and I don’t answer, please call him and let him know.”

Moral: Sometimes it is not the newspaper someone is waiting for, but the person at the door. A greeting and a brief conversation can remind someone living alone that they have not been forgotten.

Welcome Home, My Dear

Audio
Image
Text

When my late father, Hafız Ali, was fighting at Gallipoli, my mother was seven months pregnant with me. I never saw him, never knew him. Not even a photograph of him remained.

Those were terrible years: mobilization, occupation, the struggle for independence, poverty and hardship.

Our childhood passed in want, with the constant worry of finding enough to eat.

Whenever my mother went out, she would come to me first.

“Son, I’m going to the market. If your father comes home, let me know at once!”

“I’m going to your aunt’s. If your father comes home, let me know at once!”

“I’m going next door. If your father comes home, let me know at once!”

My mother never stopped waiting for him.

I grew up, began earning a living and opened a shop. Whenever she went out, she would still stop by, tell me where she was going and add:

“If your father comes home, be sure to let me know!”

The years passed, and my mother grew old. But her habit never changed. Leaning on her walking stick, she would come to me and say:

“If your father comes home, come and fetch me!”

One day she became seriously ill and took to her bed.

In her final moments, she said goodbye to us.

“You have cared for me so well. Forgive me for any trouble I have caused you.”

Then she turned slowly towards me.

“If your father comes home, tell him, ‘Mother waited for you until the end of her life.’”

Suddenly, she raised her head and looked towards the door. A smile spread across her face.

“Welcome home, my dear. Welcome home!”

With those words, she breathed her last.

Recounted as the memories of Cevdet Alkalp, an elderly cobbler who lived in Balıkesir.

The account is attributed to Mustafa Doğru, a researcher, writer and geography teacher at Bursa Çınar Anadolu High School.

In Türkiye, March 18 marks the anniversary of the Çanakkale naval victory and a day of remembrance for the fallen. On that day in 1915, the Allied fleet’s attempt to force its way through the Dardanelles towards Istanbul was repelled.

In remembering this victory, we remember not only those who died on the battlefield, but also the families who waited years for their return.

May all those who fell at Gallipoli rest in peace. May no mother spend her life waiting for someone who will never return.

Moral: The pain of war does not end on the battlefield; sometimes it becomes a lifetime of waiting at home. We owe remembrance and respect both to those who gave their lives for their homeland and to those who waited for them.

Why shout?

Audio
Image
Text

One day during class, a teacher asked the students:

“Why do people shout when they argue?”

“Because they lose their temper,” one student replied.

“But the other person is right beside them. Why should they need to shout to be heard? Can’t they speak calmly even when they’re angry?” the teacher asked.

The students offered different answers. After listening, the teacher said:

“Think of it this way: as people argue, their hearts grow further apart. It is as though they shout to bridge that distance and make their voices reach each other. Yet the louder they become, the further apart their hearts drift.”

After a brief pause, the teacher continued:

“Now think of people who love each other. They often speak softly because their hearts are close. As that closeness deepens, even a whisper is enough. Sometimes they need no words at all: a glance is enough to understand each other.”

Then the teacher addressed the class:

“When you argue, do not let your hearts drift apart. Do not say things that widen the distance between you. One day, that distance may become so great that finding your way back to each other is difficult.”

Moral: In an argument, try not only to be heard, but also to listen. Patience and respect keep hearts close; hurtful words drive them further apart.
