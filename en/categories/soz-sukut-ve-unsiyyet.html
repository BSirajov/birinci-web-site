Silent corridor

Image
Text

There were two organizations working together — one was the sponsor, the other was the project development agency.

The project team worked tirelessly for months, carefully polishing the documents, and one day, when everything was ready, they sent the materials to the sponsor and began to wait for a response. Days passed, weeks passed. The result — silence.

The uncertainty within the team grew, spreading like a shadow along the corridor:

“Have the documents arrived? Have they been reviewed?”

No one had any clear information. And requests to the sponsor only brought vague, vague answers.

A few months later, the team decided to go directly to the sponsor’s office to clarify the matter. There, the truth finally emerged:

“Yes, the documents arrived. But we did not accept them. Because the submission form was incorrect and the official procedure was not followed.”

The team froze in place.

“Why didn’t you tell us that?” they asked.

“We could have fixed everything in a short time.”...

Frustrated, the employees returned to the office and complained to their management:

“If we had been actively monitoring the process, checking in on the status regularly, and communicating clearly, this misunderstanding would never have happened. We wasted time, wasted energy, and lost trust—all because the hallway between us was silent.”

Moral: When communication is unclear, even the best work can go unnoticed. When there is no consistent follow-up, even the strongest partnership can falter. In collaboration, the greatest enemy is often not failure—it is silence.

Bakhtiyar Sirajov

Image
Text

Tell what you know, but don't teach wisdom. Listen carefully to what is said, but don't assume everything is true.

Just as being silent does not mean that you know nothing, talking too much does not mean that you know much. It is foolish to belittle a person, and to extol him excessively is cowardice.

Moral: Speak correctly, listen correctly, and you will be able to remain silent. Neither humiliate nor exalt a person - see him as he is.

The Conversation of the Candles

Image
Text

Four candles were burning in a room. The atmosphere was so quiet that you could hear the candles talking to each other.

The first candle said: “I am FAITH! But no one is helping me to burn. I think I will soon go out.”

And so it was. Its flame began to fade rapidly, and finally FAITH went out completely.

The second candle said: “I am FAITH! What a pity, I am no longer needed by anyone. That is why there is no point in me burning anymore.”

As soon as he finished speaking, a light breeze blew out FAITH completely.

The turn came for the third candle. He said in a sad voice: “I am LOVE! I have no strength left to burn anymore. People have forgotten me, my value has disappeared. Even my closest people have forgotten to love.”

LOVE could not stand it any longer and went out.

At that moment, a child entered the room and saw that all three candles were not burning. “Why don't you burn? Weren't you supposed to burn forever?” he began to cry.

Then the fourth candle spoke: “Don't be afraid, as long as I burn, we can light the other candles again. I AM HOPE!”

The child was happy and with shining eyes, he took the HOPE candle in his hand and lit the other candles one by one.

Moral: When faith, loyalty and love go out, it is hope that lights them up again. If we keep the light of hope alive, the other values ​​will also come alive.

Image
Text

(Chinese Proverb)

The one who does not know and knows that he does not know — yearns for knowledge. Teach him!

The one who knows and does not know that he knows — is asleep. Wake him up!

The one who does not know and does not know that he does not know — is ignorant. Stay away from him!

The one who knows and knows that he knows — is a leader. Follow him!

Moral: Teach the one who yearns to learn, awaken the one who is heedless, stay away from the ignorant who thinks he knows everything, and follow the one who really knows.

The Shepherd's Word

Image
Text

Once upon a time, in a village, there lived a poor but honest shepherd named Hasan. One day, the village chief called him and said:

— Hey Hasan, I have heard that you are very familiar with the pastures of the mountains. I will entrust you with the herd this summer. If my animals return healthy and fat in the fall, I will give you a cow.

Hasan agreed. He grazed his herd all summer, rain or shine, and guarded it without blinking an eye at night. When fall came, the herd returned to the village fatter and healthier than before.

Hasan came to the chief, hoping to receive the promised cow. The chief smiled mockingly and said:

— I had no intention of giving you a cow, that word was just said to encourage you to work. If you are already poor, what will you do with a cow?

Hasan's eyes filled with tears. But he answered calmly and with dignity:

— You are right, chief, I am poor. But I have a conscience and I am the master of my word. You may be rich, but if your word is worthless, then you are truly poor.

These words spread from mouth to mouth in the village. The master's name fell out of respect, and people began to avoid doing business with him. But he understood, albeit late: the value of a person is determined not by his wealth or position, but by his loyalty to his word.

Moral: A person's true wealth is not his wealth, but his word, conscience, and trust.

Words and silence

Image
Text

Sometimes a person says more by keeping quiet. Because words are not just sounds — they are a mirror of intention.

If a word will break a heart, if a sentence will extinguish hope — it is not worth saying.

If the words you are going to say will not warm a heart, if they will not cause a smile — be silent.

Remember... not every word blooms. Some are like thorns — they hurt the one who touches them.

Silence is wisdom. Sometimes it is the most valuable answer.

If a word is born from the heart, it turns into wisdom; if it comes out of the mouth without thought, it causes harm, wounds.

Silence. Because silence also speaks — sometimes more deeply, sometimes more honestly.

Moral: Not every word blooms; some hurt like thorns. Silence is wiser than words that will not bring good.

You need to call the shepherd

Image
Text

A student who wants to complete his education after many years turns to his master to receive his blessing.

His master:

— Yes, but I have one condition. I will ask you a question to see if you have properly learned the knowledge I have given you over the years. If you answer the question correctly, I will know that my efforts have not been in vain, and I will give you my blessing.

The student confidently says:

— Come on, my master, ask.

His master:

— My son, imagine that you are going to your village and on the way a pack of rabid dogs belonging to a shepherd attacks you. You are in danger. What will you do to save your life?

— I will bend down and pick up a few stones from the ground and throw them at the dogs, and I will chase them away.

— But, my son, if you throw stones at one, you throw stones at two — you will not be able to handle them all. They will join together and tear you apart.

— Then I will run away.

— Be sure that they will run away from you quickly and catch you. So what?

The student realizes that he is helpless and asks his master for help.

His master:

— My son, there is only one way out of this situation: you must call the shepherd and ask him to stop his dogs. The dogs will freeze in place as soon as they hear the shepherd's voice.

Moral: Not every problem can be solved by force. Sometimes taking a step back and looking at it differently is the shortest way out.
