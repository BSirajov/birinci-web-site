A loved one is a person who gives his love to others

Audio
Image
Text

Many years ago in China, a girl named Li-Li got married and started living in the same house with her husband and mother-in-law. However, she soon realized that it was very difficult to make ends meet with her mother-in-law. The two have completely different personalities, which causes them to argue frequently. According to Chinese traditions, this is not a pleasant situation and leads to discontent among those around her.

A few months later, the endless quarrels between the daughter-in-law and the mother-in-law turn into hellish torment for both the young woman and her husband. Believing that something must be done, the young woman runs to her father's old friend, a spice merchant, and tells him about her troubles. The old man prepares a medicine from herbs and instructs her to add a little bit of it to the food she cooks for her mother-in-law every day for three months. The poison will be given little by little so that it will not be known that the daughter-in-law killed her mother-in-law.

The old man instructs the young woman to treat her mother-in-law very well and to cook the most delicious dishes for her so that no one, especially her husband, suspects. Returning home with joy, Li-Li applied exactly what the old man had said.

Every day, she cooked the most delicious dishes and dripped “poison” into her mother-in-law’s bowl. She treated her very well so that no one would suspect anything. After a while, her mother-in-law had changed a lot, and she looked at her as her own daughter. Feeling a heavy burden, the young woman went to the spice shop, feeling regretful and begging the old man for a medicine that would cleanse the blood of the poison she had given her mother-in-law. She no longer wanted the old woman to die.

The old man looked at Li-Li standing in front of him and started laughing and said: “Dear Li-Li, what I gave you was only vitamins. The real poison was in your own brain. As you treated her well, that poison melted and gave way to love; thus you became a real mother and child.”

Moral: The real poison is often in our own brains. As you treat others with love, hatred melts, and enemies turn into friends.

Advice for Those Who Marry

Audio
Image
Text

Wise elders offered the following counsel to a young couple beginning married life:

“Do not compare your marriage with anyone else’s. Do not say, ‘Look at what that person’s husband is like,’ or ‘Look at what that person’s wife is like.’

Do not seek perfection; none of us is flawless. Do not investigate each other’s past—it is called the past for a reason.

Do not expect your spouse to read your mind. No one can. Speak openly about your wishes and concerns, and try to resolve difficulties through conversation.

Accept that alongside your shared life, each of you has a private world. Hedgehogs draw only as close as their spines allow; if they move too near, they hurt one another. Respect each other’s personal boundaries.

In marriage, be neither owner nor servant, but companions on the same road.

Do not promise, ‘Everything will always be wonderful.’ Say instead, ‘When life becomes difficult, I will be beside you.’

Even if you have been upset with each other during the day, remember to say good night before sleeping. Let tomorrow begin with reconciliation.”

Moral: A happy marriage rests not on comparison or the pursuit of perfection, but on open communication, mutual respect, and care for each other’s personal boundaries.

Don't let your hearts separate

Audio
Image
Text

Why do people shout when they argue? If the person is right next to you, why do you need to shout? Can't you talk to him calmly? Why do you shout when you get angry?

When people are unhappy with each other and argue, their hearts move away from each other. To overcome this distance and hear each other, they have to shout. The more angry they are, the louder they shout.

But what happens when people fall in love?

They don't shout, but they speak softly. Because their hearts are very close, and the distance between them is very small.

What happens when their love grows stronger?

They are silent, but they whisper to each other, and this love brings them even closer. In the end, even whispering becomes unnecessary for them - they just look at each other and understand everything without words. This happens only between two people who love each other.

Therefore, when you argue, don't let your hearts separate, don't say words that can increase the distance between you. Because one day this distance may become so great that you cannot return.

Moral: Screaming is the sound of the distance between hearts. Do not say words in an argument that will increase that distance, otherwise the return will become difficult.

Early Marriage

Audio
Image
Text

I had just graduated from university and started working as a teacher at a school. One day, I was sitting in the teachers' room, reviewing the students' exam papers. I was correcting the wrong answers of the female students so that they could get a good grade in order to move up to the next grade.

At that moment, a person entered the teachers' room and curiously asked why I was changing the girls' exam papers. I said, "If these girls fail the exam, their fathers will take them out of school and marry them off at the age of twelve or thirteen. But if they move up to the next grade, their marriageable age will be at least fifteen or sixteen. During this time, even the laziest student will definitely learn something in class."

About half an hour later, our principal called me over and asked what I was doing in the teachers' room. I said that I was reviewing the exam papers.

— Did you meet someone in the teachers' room? What did you say to him? — he asked.

— Did that person complain to you about me? — I asked.

— Not at all! The man came to me and said that he had come to inspect the school, but there was no need for it.

— Why? — I was surprised.

— Because you have a wise young teacher. Please convey my thanks to him, — he said.

Moral: A teacher's small care can change the fate of a girl. Early marriage deprives children of education; let's not turn a blind eye to this.

I Love You

Audio
Image
Text

They say a nightingale fell in love with a white rose… Every night he would go and perch beside her and declare his love. But the white rose ignored his feelings and paid no heed to his devotion.

One day the nightingale gathered all his courage, perched beside the white rose again, and said he loved her madly. But the rose snapped: “Leave me alone — I don’t love you!” and hurt him. However much he insisted, the rose rejected him.

The love was so strong that the nightingale, shaken by despair, begged the rose’s forgiveness, pulled a thorn from her, drove it into his own heart with all his strength, and dyed the pure white rose red with his blood!

The rose understood, though late, that the nightingale had truly loved her. But what good was it? It was already too late. Under the branches of the rose now dyed red, the nightingale closed his eyes forever.

Since that day, red roses have meant “I LOVE YOU.”

Moral: True love is sometimes understood too late. Value the one who loves you in the moment — fear having to say “too late.”

Purple chepken

Audio
Image
Text

In ancient times, there was a custom in Turkish villages: a purple chepken was added to the dowry of girls getting married, along with other items. The chepken was placed at the very bottom of the dowry chest, and it was hoped that the woman would never wear it.

A purple chepken was a garment that could be a threat to men. Because if a woman wore that chepken and went out onto the roof of the house, the chimney, the village square - a place where everyone could see it, it would mean "I don't love my husband, my husband is hurting me, I got married against my will, I want to get a divorce, I am in a difficult situation, help me." In such a case, the village people would help the woman, but would condemn her husband. This was considered a great shame for the man.

For this reason, men would try to solve their problems with their wives without resorting to violence, with love and respect, and, moreover, by making important concessions to the woman. Otherwise, they knew that the woman would wear a purple turban.

A man whose wife wore a purple turban would not only be forced to divorce, but would also not be able to easily remarry. Because everyone knew that a man who made his wife wear a purple turban would never become a husband. No one would give a girl to a man who did not respect a woman and was violent, and it would be very difficult for him to remarry. It is said that this is why the number of women subjected to violence was almost non-existent.

If a woman wore a purple turban, then the flowing waters would stop, the hands that milked cows, spun wool, and wove rugs would stop; old mothers and grandmothers would stop their work and come to the defense of the woman who was forced to wear a purple turban.

At that time, if there were no weddings, no flutes, no other entertainment around, everyone would be silent. Because for a married woman to wear a purple turban or fall into a difficult situation was considered death.

The husband of a woman wearing a purple turban cannot leave the house, cannot go to the teahouse, and no one will look at his face.

Moral: Violence against women is unacceptable. When society considers it a duty of conscience to protect women, there is no room for oppression.

Take Care of Those You Love

Audio
Image
Text

On a sunny day in the park, a woman told the man beside her that the boy in the red shirt on the swings was her son. The man smiled and pointed to the boy in blue: that was his son, Jamil.

After a while, he checked his watch and called:

“Jamil, it is time to go home.”

“Five more minutes, Dad, please!”

The father agreed. Later he called again, but the boy asked for another five minutes. Once more, the man said yes.

The woman remarked admiringly:

“You are a very patient father.”

The man answered with a sorrowful smile:

“This is not patience. Last year my older son Kamal was struck nearby by a drunk driver. I had never given him enough of my time. Now I would give anything for five more minutes with him. I promised not to repeat that mistake with Jamil”.

“My son thinks he gains five more minutes of play. In truth, I am the one who gains — the happiness of watching him five minutes longer.”

Moral: Every extra moment with those you love is a gift. Before it is too late, give them five more minutes today.

The eraser in marriage

Audio
Image
Text

A story takes place between a father and his newly married young son.

The father asks his son to bring a white piece of paper, a pen, and an eraser.

— Why? — the son asks.

— You bring it, — says the father.

The son brings a pen and paper, but he can’t find an eraser.

— Go and get an eraser, — says the father.

The son is very surprised, but he goes and buys an eraser, brings it, and sits next to his father.

— Write, — says the father.

— What should I write?

— Write whatever you want.

The son writes a sentence.

— Erase it now, — says the father.

The son erases it.

— Write another sentence.

— For God’s sake, father, what do you want?

— Write something again.

The son writes. His father again erases what he has written.

After this writing and erasing has been repeated several times, the father asks:

— Is the paper still white?

— Yes, it is white, father. What does all this mean?

The father puts his hand on his son's shoulder and says:

— Yes, my son, marriage is something that cannot be done without an eraser... You need to carry an eraser with you to erase some of the things you will see and dislike in your wife. And your wife should have such an eraser to erase the unpleasant things you will do. Because if you don't do this, your marriage page will be black and white in a few days...

If you expect mutual love and respect in your life, both of you will show it to each other. And you will never forget the eraser!

Moral: Marriage is the art of forgiveness. For love and respect to continue, both parties must be able to "erase" each other's flaws.

The Mother-of-Pearl Flower

Audio
Image
Text

The situation of an eighty-year-old couple who were being tried for divorce in the courtroom was painful for everyone… The judge, looking into the eyes of the stubborn, silent grandmother, sunken from crying, addressed the old woman in a full voice:

— Tell me, aunt, why do you want a divorce? — he asked.

After taking a deep breath, the old woman began to speak in a choked voice:

— This man has worn me out; for exactly fifty years he has made me tired of life…, — she said and fell silent.

A long silence fell on the hall. The silence was broken by a journalist’s photo flash. Who knows what headline the newspapers would give this event after fifty years of life together. Many journalists were watching the trial. What would the woman say? Everyone was listening to her. The old woman’s eyes filled with tears and she continued: — I had a mother-of-pearl flower that I loved very much… He doesn’t know… It was fifty years ago. I had planted that flower by taking a leaf from the flowers he had given me. We had no children, but I considered this flower my child. After a while, I saw that the flower had begun to wither.

At that time, I promised that I would wake up every night before sunrise and water this flower. For fifty years this man never once got up at night and said, “Let me water this flower.” Until last night… Last night I lay down exhausted and could not wake up… I spent fifty years with such a man. I gave my life, my hope, my everything. I saw nothing from him. I assure you, I can live my life better without him.

The judge turned to the elder:

— Do you have anything to say, grandfather? — he asked.

The old man leaned on his staff, turned to the judge with a guilty expression on his face, and began to speak:

— I spent my military service as a gardener. I worked hard to make that garden grow beautifully and healthily. I met Fatima there. I gave her bouquets of the most beautiful flowers. When we were newly married, she complained of neck pain, so I took her to the doctor. The doctor said, “If she sleeps too long without waking, the muscles in her neck will stiffen and her condition will worsen. She should break her sleep every night, wake up, and walk around.” The lady did not listen to the doctor’s words… I did not listen to her either… In those days our flower accidentally started to wither. I told her, “If you get up and water the flower at night, it will come back to life.” I asked her to promise, and she promised…

I woke her up every night and watched her. I watched the woman I loved watering the flower she thought of as her child. Every night, it was as if I was that flower…

Grandfather paused for a moment and continued:

— Every night, after she went to bed, I woke up to empty the water from the pot. Mother-of-pearl does not like to be watered at night, Your Honor… And last night… what can I say, this old age… I could not wake up either… The flower could have gone thirsty, but my wife would have faced health complications… She blamed me for not waking her… I did not raise my voice; I remained silent…

At that moment everyone in the hall, including the journalists, was in tears…

Moral: Sometimes love is hidden not in words, but in the care that is silently borne for years. Let us protect those we love, and stay away from breaking hearts.

The red dress

Audio
Image
Text

The red dress was hanging in my mother's wardrobe. It seemed to shine next to the worn, faded clothes she had worn all her life.

I went to touch my mother and the moment I saw it, I realized that she was living the last moments of her life.

Seeing the red dress, I said to her:

— Mom, what a beautiful dress! I have never seen you wear it before, — I said.

— I have never worn it, — she said softly. — Sit down next to me, my beautiful daughter, I want to teach you a lesson before I leave this world.

I sat down on the edge of the bed. My mother took a deep breath. “I don’t have much time left,” she said and continued:

— While I thought I had always taught you good things, I now realize that I have taught you very wrong things.

— What is that word, mother?

— Listen to me. I have always believed that a good woman should not think of herself first. I should have thought of others before myself. I should have always fulfilled everyone's wishes, this, that. My desires were crushed under the desires of others. Who knows, maybe one day my desires would come true too. But that day never came. My whole life was spent in sacrifices—for your father, for your brothers and sisters, for you.

— That's right, mother, you did everything a mother could do.

— Oh, my daughter, what I did for you or for them didn't benefit me. Do you understand? I never wanted anything for myself! Your father is now in the next room, staring at the walls, annoyed. When the doctor told him that I was living my last days, he came to me and said:

— You must not die, do you hear me? What will I do after you? — he said.

— My daughter, I have always worked hard for you. Seven days a week... I was always the first to wake up in the house and the last to go to bed. I always ate the burnt bread, the smallest pieces. I spent every penny I had saved on your clothes, on your books—even if they were often unnecessary. I have never gone shopping and bought myself anything nice. I just bought that red dress you saw last year. I had some money saved up to fix the washing machine. When I got home with the dress, my father was very upset:

— Where are you going to wear this dress? Are you going to the opera or something? — he said.

In fact, he was right. I never wore that dress except when I tried it on in the store.

— Oh, my daughter, I used to think that if a person does not think about himself in this world, he will be happy in the next. But I don’t believe that anymore. I think God wants us to fulfill our desires in this world and now. If a miracle could happen now and I could get out of this bed, I would be a very different person.

These were my mother’s last words to me:

— My daughter, — she said, — don’t follow my path. Promise me.

I promised my mother and she breathed her last.

Moral: A person who always forgets himself will one day find that he can't live for anything. Love others, but give yourself the right to live too.

True Love

Audio
Image
Text

There was an island where all the feelings lived.

One day news spread that the island was sinking. The feelings began to prepare in haste to leave.

Love was in no hurry to leave. She waited until the last moment, as if sure a miracle would save her. When the island was almost gone, Love finally decided to ask for help.

At that moment Wealth was sailing away in a very large boat.

Love asked: “Wealth, can you take me with you?” Wealth said: “No, I can’t. My boat is full of gold and silver — there is no room for you.”

Love then turned to Arrogance, sailing in a beautiful sailboat, and asked for help: “Arrogance, please help me.”

Arrogance replied: “I can’t help you, Love. You’re too wet — you’ll ruin my sail.”

Sadness was nearby. Love asked him too: “Sadness, may I come with you?”

Sadness said: “Oh, Love, I am so sad that I need to be alone.”

Happiness was not far from Love, yet so excited that she did not hear Love’s call.

Suddenly a voice said: “Love, come with me.” Love felt so lucky and happy that it did not even occur to her to ask who was reaching out. When they reached the shore, the one who had helped Love left her there and went on.

On the shore, realizing how much she owed, Love told a wise person what had happened and asked: “Who was it that helped me?”

The wise one answered: “It was Time.”

“Time? But why did Time help me?” Love asked.

The wise one smiled: “Because only Time knows how important and precious Love is.”

Moral: Only time knows the worth of true love. Time keeps sincere love alive and lets the false fade into forgetfulness.

Moral: There can be no peace or love where a woman is not valued. It is the duty of society to see her work and heart.

Moral: Everything depends on the perspective. Those who cannot create happiness within themselves cannot take it from others - they can walk even in the rain.
