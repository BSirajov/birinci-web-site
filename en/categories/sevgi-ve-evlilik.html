Those Who Give Love Receive Love

Audio
Image
Text

Many years ago in China, a young woman named Li-Li married and moved in with her husband and mother-in-law. She soon found it difficult to get along with her mother-in-law. Their personalities were very different, and they argued frequently. These quarrels offended traditional expectations of family harmony and upset everyone around them.

After several months, the constant fighting had made life unbearable for Li-Li and her husband. Desperate to do something, she went to an old friend of her father’s, a spice merchant, and told him her troubles. He prepared an herbal mixture and told her to add a little to her mother-in-law’s food each day for three months. He said the poison would act so gradually that no one would suspect her.

To avoid arousing suspicion, especially her husband’s, he told Li-Li to treat her mother-in-law kindly and cook her favorite dishes. Delighted, Li-Li went home and followed his instructions.

Each day she prepared delicious meals and added a little of the supposed poison. She was careful to be kind, so that no one would suspect her. In time, her mother-in-law’s attitude changed, and she began treating Li-Li like her own daughter. Overcome with remorse, Li-Li returned to the spice merchant and begged him for an antidote. She no longer wanted her mother-in-law to die.

The old man laughed gently. “Dear Li-Li, all I gave you was vitamins. The real poison was in your mind. As you treated her with kindness, that poison gave way to love. Now you have become like mother and daughter.”

Moral: The real poison often lies in our own thoughts. When we treat others with love, hatred can fade and enemies can become friends.

Advice for Newlyweds

Audio
Image
Text

Wise elders offered the following counsel to a young couple beginning married life:

“Do not compare your marriage with anyone else’s. Do not say, ‘Look at what that person’s husband is like,’ or ‘Look at what that person’s wife is like.’

Do not expect perfection; none of us is flawless. Do not dig into each other’s past—it is called the past for a reason.

Do not expect your spouse to read your mind. No one can. Speak openly about your wishes and concerns, and try to resolve difficulties through conversation.

Accept that alongside your shared life, each of you has a private world. Hedgehogs draw only as close as their spines allow; if they move too near, they hurt one another. Respect each other’s personal boundaries.

In marriage, be neither owner nor servant, but companions on the same road.

Do not promise, ‘Everything will always be wonderful.’ Say instead, ‘When life becomes difficult, I will be beside you.’

Even if you have been upset with each other during the day, remember to say good night before going to sleep. Let tomorrow begin with reconciliation.”

Moral: A happy marriage rests not on comparison or the pursuit of perfection, but on open communication, mutual respect, and care for each other’s personal boundaries.

Do Not Let Your Hearts Drift Apart

Audio
Image
Text

Why do people shout when they argue? If the person is right next to you, why do you need to shout? Can't you talk to him calmly? Why do you shout when you get angry?

When people quarrel, their hearts seem to move farther apart. They raise their voices as though trying to bridge that distance. The angrier they become, the louder they shout.

But what happens when people fall in love?

They speak softly, because their hearts feel close together.

What happens when their love grows stronger?

Their voices drop to a whisper, and love draws them closer still. Eventually even whispers seem unnecessary: a look is enough for them to understand each other.

So when you argue, do not let your hearts drift apart. Avoid words that widen the distance between you. One day, it may become so great that you cannot find your way back.

Moral: Shouting reflects the distance between hearts. Even in an argument, choose words that leave a way back to each other.

Early Marriage

Audio
Image
Text

I had just graduated from university and begun teaching. One day, I was in the staff room marking exam papers. I was correcting some of the girls’ wrong answers so that they could pass and move up to the next year.

A man came in and asked why I was changing their answers. “If these girls fail,” I explained, “their fathers will take them out of school and marry them off at twelve or thirteen. If they move up, they may be able to stay until they’re fifteen or sixteen. In that time, even the least motivated student is bound to learn something.”

About half an hour later, the principal called me in and asked what I had been doing in the staff room. I told him I had been marking papers.

“Did you meet anyone there? What did you say to him?” he asked.

“Did he complain about me?” I asked.

“Quite the opposite. He told me he had come to inspect the school, but no longer felt he needed to.”

“Why not?” I asked in surprise.

“He said, ‘You have a wise young teacher. Please pass on my thanks.’”

Moral: A teacher’s care can change a girl’s future. Early marriage can rob children of their education; we must not turn a blind eye to it.

I Love You

Audio
Image
Text

They say a nightingale fell in love with a white rose… Every night he would go and perch beside her and declare his love. But the white rose ignored his feelings and paid no heed to his devotion.

One day the nightingale gathered all his courage, perched beside the white rose again, and said he loved her madly. But the rose snapped: “Leave me alone — I don’t love you!” and hurt him. However much he insisted, the rose rejected him.

The love was so strong that the nightingale, shaken by despair, begged the rose’s forgiveness, pulled a thorn from her, drove it into his own heart with all his strength, and dyed the pure white rose red with his blood!

Only then did the rose understand his love. But it was too late. Beneath her red flowers, the nightingale closed his eyes forever.

Since that day, red roses have meant “I LOVE YOU.”

Moral: Affection may go unrecognized until it is too late. Appreciate the love in your life while there is still time.

The Purple Chepken

Audio
Image
Text

According to an old account from Turkish villages, a bride’s dowry included a purple chepken, a traditional jacket. It lay at the bottom of her chest, with the hope that she would never need to wear it.

If a woman put on the jacket and stood on a roof or in the village square, it was a public appeal for help. It could mean that she had been forced to marry, that her husband mistreated her, or that she wanted to leave him. The villagers were expected to protect her, while her husband faced public shame.

The custom encouraged husbands to settle disagreements respectfully rather than through violence. They knew what it would mean if their wives wore the purple jacket.

A man whose wife made this appeal faced divorce and found it difficult to marry again. Families would not willingly entrust a daughter to someone known to mistreat women. The account credits this custom with helping to deter abuse.

When a woman appeared in the jacket, daily work stopped. Women put aside their milking, spinning, and weaving. Mothers and grandmothers gathered to defend her.

Music and celebrations fell silent. Her distress was treated with the gravity of a death in the community.

Her husband faced such disgrace that he could scarcely leave the house or show his face at the teahouse.

Moral: Violence against women is unacceptable. When society considers it a duty of conscience to protect women, there is no room for oppression.

Take Care of Those You Love

Audio
Image
Text

On a sunny day in the park, a woman told the man beside her that the boy in the red shirt on the swings was her son. The man smiled and pointed to the boy in blue: that was his son, Jamil.

After a while, he checked his watch and called:

“Jamil, it is time to go home.”

“Five more minutes, Dad, please!”

The father agreed. Later he called again, but the boy asked for another five minutes. Once more, the man said yes.

The woman remarked admiringly:

“You are a very patient father.”

The man answered with a sorrowful smile:

“This isn’t patience. Last year my older son, Kamal, was hit nearby by a drunk driver. I had never given him enough of my time. Now I would give anything for five more minutes with him. I promised myself I wouldn’t make the same mistake with Jamil.

“He thinks he’s getting five more minutes to play. But I’m the one receiving a gift: five more minutes of happiness watching him.”

Moral: Every extra moment with those you love is a gift. Before it is too late, give them five more minutes today.

An Eraser for Marriage

Audio
Image
Text

A father wanted to share a lesson with his newly married son.

The father asked his newly married son for a sheet of paper, a pencil, and an eraser.

“Why?”

“Just bring them.”

The son found paper and a pencil, but no eraser.

“Then go and buy one.”

Puzzled, the son bought an eraser and sat down beside him.

“Write something.”

“What should I write?”

“Anything you like.”

The son wrote a sentence.

“Now erase it.”

He erased it.

“Write another sentence.”

“Father, please tell me what this is about.”

“Write one more.”

The son wrote again, and his father asked him to erase it.

After repeating this several times, the father asked:

“Is the paper still white?”

“Yes. But what does that mean?”

His father rested a hand on his shoulder.

“A marriage needs an eraser too, son. You will both make mistakes and do things the other dislikes. Each of you must be willing to erase some of those marks. Otherwise, the page you share will soon be covered with them.

“If you want love and respect, offer them to each other. And remember the eraser.”

Moral: Marriage is the art of forgiveness. For love and respect to continue, both parties must be able to "erase" each other's flaws.

The Mother-of-Pearl Flower

Audio
Image
Text

An elderly couple, both in their eighties, stood before a divorce court after fifty years of marriage. The woman’s eyes were swollen from crying. The judge gently asked:

“Why do you want a divorce?”

After taking a deep breath, the old woman began to speak in a choked voice:

“He has worn me down,” she said, her voice breaking. “For fifty years, he has made me weary of life.”

The courtroom fell silent, interrupted only by a photographer’s flash. Reporters waited to hear what she would say. “I have a mother-of-pearl plant that I love dearly. I grew it from a leaf taken from flowers he gave me fifty years ago. We never had children, so I cared for it like a child. One day, it began to wither.

“I promised to get up before dawn every night to water it. In fifty years, he never once offered to do it for me. Last night, I was so exhausted that I couldn’t wake up. I’ve given this man my life, my hopes, everything, and received nothing in return. I would be better off alone.”

The judge turned to the elder:

“Would you like to say anything?”

Leaning on his cane, the husband began:

“I was a gardener during my military service. That was where I met Fatima. I used to bring her the finest flowers I could grow. Soon after we married, she developed neck pain. A doctor told us that sleeping too long without moving made it worse. He advised her to get up and walk around during the night, but she wouldn’t listen to either of us. Then the plant began to wither. I told her it would recover if she got up at night to water it. She promised she would.

“Every night, I woke her and watched her tend the plant she loved like a child. Seeing her care for it, I felt as though I were that flower.”

He paused, then continued:

“After she went back to bed, I got up to empty the excess water from the pot. The plant didn’t need all that water, Your Honor. Last night, I failed to wake too. We’re getting old. Missing its water wouldn’t harm the plant, but I worried about my wife. She blamed me for not waking her. I didn’t argue.”

At that moment everyone in the hall, including the journalists, was in tears…

Moral: Love is sometimes expressed through years of quiet care. Recognize that care, and remember to speak gently to those you love.

The Red Dress

Audio
Image
Text

The red dress was hanging in my mother's wardrobe. It seemed to shine next to the worn, faded clothes she had worn all her life.

When I came to my mother’s bedside, I could see that she was nearing the end of her life.

Seeing the red dress, I said to her:

“What a beautiful dress, Mom. I’ve never seen you wear it.”

“I never have,” she said softly. “Sit beside me, my dear. There’s something I want to tell you before I go.”

I sat down on the edge of the bed. My mother took a deep breath. “I don’t have much time left,” she said and continued:

“I thought I had always set you a good example. Now I’m afraid I taught you something very wrong.”

“What do you mean, Mom?”

“I believed a good woman should always put everyone else first. Their wishes mattered; mine could wait. I kept thinking my turn would come, but it never did. My whole life became a sacrifice for your father and all of you children.”

“But you did everything for us.”

“Yes, and I forgot to want anything for myself. Your father is sitting in the next room, upset and staring at the walls. When the doctor told him I was dying, he came in and said:

“‘You can’t die. What am I supposed to do without you?’

“I worked seven days a week, the first up and the last to bed. I ate the burned toast and the smallest portions. Every spare coin went on your clothes and books, even things you didn’t really need. I never bought myself anything lovely until last year, when I used the money saved for a washing-machine repair to buy that red dress. Your father was furious.

“‘Where do you think you’ll wear that? To the opera?’ he asked.

“And there it stayed. The only time I ever wore it was in the shop.

“I used to think that denying myself everything here would bring happiness in the next world. Now I believe Allah wants us to enjoy life here too. If I could get out of this bed, I would live differently.”

These were my mother’s last words to me:

“Promise me you won’t do what I did.”

I promised my mother and she breathed her last.

Moral: Loving others should not mean abandoning your own life. Leave room for your needs and hopes too.

True Love

Audio
Image
Text

There was an island where all the feelings lived.

One day news spread that the island was sinking. The feelings began to prepare in haste to leave.

Love was in no hurry to leave. She waited until the last moment, as if sure a miracle would save her. When the island was almost gone, Love finally decided to ask for help.

At that moment Wealth was sailing away in a very large boat.

Love asked: “Wealth, can you take me with you?” Wealth said: “No, I can’t. My boat is full of gold and silver — there is no room for you.”

Love then turned to Arrogance, sailing in a beautiful sailboat, and asked for help: “Arrogance, please help me.”

Arrogance replied, “You’re soaking wet. You’ll spoil my beautiful boat.”

Sadness was nearby. Love asked him too: “Sadness, may I come with you?”

Sadness said: “Oh, Love, I am so sad that I need to be alone.”

Happiness was not far from Love, yet so excited that she did not hear Love’s call.

Suddenly a voice said: “Love, come with me.” Love felt so lucky and happy that it did not even occur to her to ask who was reaching out. When they reached the shore, the one who had helped Love left her there and went on.

On the shore, realizing how much she owed, Love told a wise person what had happened and asked: “Who was it that helped me?”

The wise one answered: “It was Time.”

“Time? But why did Time help me?” Love asked.

The wise one smiled: “Because only Time knows how important and precious Love is.”

Moral: Only time knows the worth of true love. Time keeps sincere love alive and lets the false fade into forgetfulness.

What Women Have Endured

Audio
Image
Text

What have women endured in this world?

Even in fairy tales, they made her either a witch or a stepmother...

Stories burdened women with every accusation.

A woman was rarely allowed to be herself, though she gave life, nurtured it, and loved without conditions.

Woman...

They told her that her hair was long and her mind was short.

“Your hands are covered in flour. Stay in the kitchen. Don’t interfere.”

Woman, how much you have endured.

Few people knew your worth.

Few people could see your love, your heart, your goodness.

They called you wicked when you asked for your rights and virtuous when you kept silent.

Some mistook your love for a wish to control them.

Sometimes, those you raised to the heavens with your love preferred to stay there and forgot about you.

They did not ask how you were or whether you needed help.

They scarcely noticed whether you were there.

They forgot that you had a soul.

They forgot that you were human.

They wore you down, crushed your spirit, and ignored you.

Yet you wanted only to help, to care, and to make life better.

Your soul wandered in this world in need of love.

They talked about heaven and made you experience hell.

They looked into your eyes and denied the pain they saw. They would not hear the cry within you.

You loved being a mother, looking into your child’s eyes. In that child’s embrace, you found an affection you could trust.

Moral: There can be no peace or love where a woman is not valued. It is the duty of society to see her work and heart.

Can a Broken Heart Love Again?

Audio
Image
Text

A man asked a dervish:

“Can a broken heart love again?”

“Yes, it can,” the dervish replied.

“But have you ever drunk water from a broken vessel?” the man asked.

“Tell me,” said the dervish, “have you ever given up drinking water because a vessel broke?”

Moral: Even if the heart is broken, it does not stop loving. Just as one does not stop drinking water because a vessel is broken, so too does a person continue to love.

The Woman and the Mirror

Audio
Image
Text

A woman wakes up in the morning and looks in the mirror. She sees that she has three strands of hair left on her head.

“I’ll braid my hair today,” she decided. She did, and had a lovely day.

The next day she wakes up and looks in the mirror again... She sees that she has two strands of hair left on her head.

“I’ll part it down the middle.” She did, and had another lovely day.

The next day she wakes up and sees that she has only one strand of hair left on her head.

“A ponytail today!” she said cheerfully, and enjoyed her day again.

Another day she looks in the mirror and sees that she has no hair left on her head.

“Wonderful!” she exclaimed. “Today I won’t have to do my hair at all.”

Moral: We cannot control every change, but our outlook can help us meet it with good humor.
