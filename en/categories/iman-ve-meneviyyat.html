Church bell

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

In ancient times, there was a rule in a country: when a commoner died, the church bell was rung once, when a nobleman died twice, when one of the king's relatives died three times, and when the king himself died four times.

One day, a court where everyone sought justice unjustly convicted a citizen, and on this occasion the church bell was rung five times.

The people came to the church in confusion and asked: "Father, who is more important than the king that the church bell was rung five times?"

The priest replied: "Of course there is - justice. Today justice has died."

Moral: When justice dies, something greater than the king dies. Stand by the truth so that injustice does not go unpunished.

Confidence is half the battle!

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

A school principal invited several of his fellow teachers into his office and said to them:

— Since you are our best, most capable teachers, we are entrusting you with a group of the most intelligent and capable students we have selected. Your task is to provide them with a very good education and raise them to a higher level over the next year. I wish you success.

Teachers, students, and parents considered this initiative a very good idea and were eagerly awaiting the end of the school year.

Finally, the school year ended. After the tests, it turned out that those students had shown higher results than other schoolchildren in the city.

The principal congratulated the teachers and said: “I have a confession to make. Those students we entrusted to you were not the most capable children at all - they were average. We chose them by chance.”

Hearing this, the teachers became proud and began to consider the students’ success a result of their professionalism.

The principal continued: “I also have a confession. You weren't the most capable teachers either! I picked your names at random from a bag of papers I had stuffed into a bag. You succeeded because you believed.”

Moral: Belief awakens the hidden power within a person; and high expectations are sometimes the first reason for high results.

Dervish Spoons

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

One day, the dervish was asked:

— What is the difference between true lovers and those who only talk about love?

— Wait, I will show you now, — said the dervish.

He called several so-called lovers and set a table for them. After the guests took their places, hot soup was brought to the table, followed by unusually long spoons called “dervish spoons”. The dervish stated his condition:

— You will eat your soup by holding the ends of these spoons.

The people agreed. But the spoons were so long that it was impossible to bring the soup to their mouths — with every attempt, the soup spilled on them, and the people burned themselves. As a result, they left the table hungry and embarrassed.

Then the dervish called those who knew what true love was. People with radiant faces and eyes full of love came and sat down at the table. The dervish gave them the same condition. This time the scene was different: everyone filled their spoons and handed them to the person in front of them, and one person fed the other. The table was filled with joy, and everyone stood up with full bellies and gratitude.

After the guests dispersed, the dervish turned to the questioners:

— Have you seen it? Whoever sees only himself at the table of life remains hungry. Whoever thinks of his brother or friend and extends his hand to him is also satisfied. In the market of life, it is not always the buyer who benefits, but the giver.

Moral: Whoever thinks only of himself remains hungry at the table of life, while whoever feeds the person in front of him is also satisfied. The giver always wins.

Dervish's Robe

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

One day, a wounded bird came to the king and complained that a dervish had broken its wing. The king immediately summoned the dervish.

— This bird is complaining about you. Why did you break its wing? — he asked.

The dervish tried to defend himself:

— Thank Allah, the king, I wanted to hunt this bird. I approached it, but it did not run away. I went up to it, but it did not move again. Thinking that it would surrender and trying to catch it, it tried to run away at the moment when I wanted to catch it — and its wing was broken.

The king turned his face to the bird:

— This man is right. Why did you not run away? It did not attack it suddenly. You could have seen it approaching and flown away in time. And now you come to me and complain.

The bird tried to justify itself:

— I did not run away because I saw it in the guise of a dervish. If it were a hunter, I would have flown away immediately. But I thought, "The dervish will not harm you; they fear Allah."

The king was silent for a moment, then he ordered:

— The bird is right. Break the dervish's arm!

The bird immediately protested:

Please, don't do that!

— Why? — the king asked.

— If you break his arm, he will do the same thing again after he recovers. It is best to have him remove his dervish cloak so that birds like me will not be deceived again.

Moral: Outward holiness does not make a person genuine. Look at the actions, not the clothes - clothes do not make a person a dervish.

Do your part and put your trust in Allah

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

Once upon a time, there was a dervish. He spent most of his days in solitude and worship. However, he did not like being in need of people, so he would devote part of his day to work: he would work a little, buy bread with the money he earned with his hands, and distribute the excess to the poor.

One day, while he was still engaged in worship in the desert, his eyes fell on a fox. The fox's legs were crippled, and he could barely move forward. The dervish thought to himself: "I wonder what this fox does for a living? Wild animals live on hunting, and for hunting they need sharp feet. This poor thing has no feet. I wonder where Allah provides for him?"

While he was thinking this, he saw a lion from afar bringing a gazelle he had hunted in its tusks. The lion puts down its prey, tears it apart, eats it to its heart's content, then leaves the rest there and walks away. The crippled fox, who had been lying in wait for a while, crawls towards it, eats the meat and fills its stomach.

The dervish is amazed at this. He praises Allah, who provides sustenance to the fox through the lion's hand. Then the following thought comes to his mind: "Since this fox earns its sustenance without doing anything, why shouldn't Allah provide for me? I cut off the hours I would have set aside for worship and spend them on work. However, I can also worship my Lord during those hours and earn rewards. From now on, I will not work. Allah, who sent the crippled fox's food to the lion's feet, will also provide for me in some way."

From that day on, the dervish abandons work and devotes himself to worship day and night. But no matter how much he waits, sustenance does not come. No one knocks on his door, no one brings him a piece of bread. The dervish melts away day by day, leaving only skin and bones.

Finally, one day when he was exhausted, he heard a voice from the unseen:

— I gave you a healthy body, strong arms, and sharp legs. But you have compared yourself to a crippled fox. Why do you compare yourself to a fox and not a lion? Why do you look at someone else's surplus? Get up, work like a lion, earn your own livelihood. Let the helpless and the helpless rely on you. Eat yourself and feed the needy.

The dervish is ashamed of what he has done, repents, and returns to his previous life. He earns his livelihood again with the labor of his own hands, and gives up the idea of ​​being a burden to others once and for all.

Moral: A person who looks at the share of the needy despite having the strength is not trusting in Allah - he is simply covering up his laziness with faith.

Drinking the syrup of martyrdom

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

During the Ottoman period, soldiers who were seriously injured in wars and were dying would drink syrup made from Zamzam water from small barrels they carried with them, so that their mouths would be sweetened before they became martyrs. The expression “drinking the syrup of martyrdom” originated from here.

Moral: Behind every expression, there is a history, a sacrifice. The words in our language carry the memory of our past.

Forty days

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

There is a custom in Turkic countries where the mother and baby do not leave the house for forty days after birth, and no guests come to their house. It is said that this is an ancient Turkish custom and dates back to the Goyturks.

When the time came, the child's fortieth birthday was celebrated as follows: the mother and child were invited to the house of one of their close relatives, and the owner of the house would give gifts in the name of the baby - SMALL MONEY so that he would be prosperous throughout his life; COTTON so that he would live long and have a soft heart until his hair turned gray; SUGAR so that his mouth would always be sweet and his face would always smile; WHEAT so that his house would be blessed; SALT so that his mouth would not run out of flavor; EGGS so that his stomach would be full and his back strong.

Moral: A blessing and love are hidden in each of our customs. The gifts given to a baby are actually beautiful wishes that are kept for his whole life.

Haram money

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

One day, while walking on the road, a sum of money fell from the hand of a person walking in front of me. Usually, in such cases, I would warn him by saying, “You dropped your money,” but this time I followed the devil, picked up the money from the ground and put it in my pocket.

When I got home, I told my wife the story.

— Put some more on top, let's go to the cinema, — she said. “Let's do it.” I said. We decided to go to the cinema on the weekend.

The lady insisted: “You promise now, but then you suddenly refuse; it's better to buy the tickets online now so that we can be sure.” I said “Let's do it” and bought the tickets online.

I am a repairman myself. A paid client of mine called and said, “Come to my garden on Saturday, we have some work to do.” I asked, “Can I come on Sunday?”, but my client said, “No, it's not possible.” Since we bought the cinema tickets on Saturday, as if they were the other way around, I lost a lot of money from this.

In short, Saturday arrived. As my wife and I were getting ready to leave the house, we unexpectedly met my mother-in-law and father-in-law in front of the door.

I was about to say, “We’re going to the movies,” and continue on our way, when my wife quickly moved away from me and said, “We’re going to the movies, come on, come with us.” I realized how helpless I was.

Since my mother-in-law doesn’t like public transportation, we had to take a taxi to the cinema. Of course, I had to pay for the taxi.

We got to the cinema. I bought two extra tickets. Moreover, I had to buy a box of popcorn for everyone before the movie started — it couldn’t be otherwise, the groom has to entertain everyone, because he found money on the way! I didn’t even spend as much money on the sacrificial meat as I had spent on that popcorn. Since we quickly emptied the popcorn boxes, I had to go buy new ones during the movie. In short, I paid a lot for the money I found, but my punishment wasn’t over yet. Well, what we were going to see was still ahead of us.

Finally, the movie ended, and we went out into the street. We went out, but what a way! I ran into one of my old acquaintances. Since we had a long-standing enmity, my father-in-law and mother-in-law were with me, but he said a few harsh words to me. I couldn't stand it. A fight broke out. It broke out, it broke out! I hit him, he hit me. My father-in-law also got involved. In short, by the time the police arrived, my nose and my father-in-law's arm were broken. Although we ended our fight peacefully at the police station, I had to pay for both my and my father-in-law's hospital expenses due to the injuries we received.

Recently, I was walking along the road again. I saw someone buying doner in front of me. When the seller was giving me the change, the coins fell to the ground. The man bent down and didn't pick up the change. I couldn't bear it and said, “Oh brother, at least don't think about yourself, think about the person who will find this money; ”Don't put people in trouble, please take that money from the ground.“”

Moral: Money that is not earned by the sweat of your brow does not benefit a person; often you lose much more than you earn. Do not touch what is not lawful.

Light of the Universe

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

British artist William Holman Hunt's "Light of the Universe" is on display at the Royal Academy in London. The painting depicts a man standing at a door at night with a lantern in one hand, looking philosophical. The man knocks on the door with the other hand, hoping that it will open.

An art critic who studied the painting asked Hunt: "It's a beautiful painting, but I don't understand its meaning. Will the door that the man is knocking on never open? Perhaps you forgot to pull the doorknob?"

Hunt smiled and replied: "This is no ordinary door. This door embodies the human heart. Since the door only opens from the inside, it has no outside handle. If that door is not opened for you from the inside, you cannot enter."

Moral: The door of the heart opens only from the inside. Love and goodness do not force their way in - you must let them in yourself.

My Alif Has Been Dotted

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

There is an expression in Turkish: “elifim noktalandı.” It comes from writing the letters “alif” (ا) and “ha” (ه) side by side in the old Ottoman script (اه). Because the “ha” after “alif” resembles a dot, people say “alifim noktalandı,” and metaphorically it means “my joy turned to sorrow.” The letter “alif,” with its long, thin appearance, expresses freedom and beauty, while the letter “ha,” with its bent, almost crushed appearance, expresses distress and collapse.

When these two letters come together, the cry “ah” appears.

Moral: Sometimes the change of a letter — of a moment — turns joy into sadness. Every expression in our language carries the trace of an emotion, a story.

Not to deviate from the right path

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

One day, a man who was wasting his time in idleness, idleness, and eating inheritances got tired of this situation and went to the king to seek advice on how to live a righteous life without deviating from the right path and asked him to show him a way. The king thought for a while and ordered the man to do the following: "Take this jar full of olive oil and carry it from one end of the city to the other without spilling even a drop. If a single drop falls on the ground, know that your neck will be cut off." He also assigned two soldiers to the man to supervise.

The man gathered all his strength, used his intelligence, and carried the jar to the other end of the city without spilling even a drop. Then he returned and went to the king again and said, "May the king live, I have carried out your order perfectly." The king asked, "What did you see in the city, what did you witness?" There was a market in the city that day, and the crowd was so large that even a pin would not fall to the ground.

Nevertheless, the man replied to the king: "May the king live, I was so careful not to spill the oil in the jar on the ground for the sake of my life that I could not take my eyes off the jar for a moment to look around. For this reason, I did not see anyone or witness any event."

After this lesson, the king gave the man his advice with peace of mind: "If you are so careful in everything you do, in every task you are given, if you devote yourself to your work, and if you do not forget that Allah is always watching over you, you will never stray from the right path."

Moral: A person who approaches everything he does with full attention, and who does not forget that Allah is watching him, will never stray from the right path.

Sacrificial meat

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

I was in the 5th grade of school. We didn't need anyone. My father, who was a builder, had joined a cooperative that was under construction. We dreamed that “it won't take six or seven months, and we will move into our own house.”

One morning we woke up to a terrible shaking. There had been an earthquake. Thankfully, nothing bad had happened to us or to those around us. No one in our building was hurt. But although our house and our lives were safe, this event brought another trouble to our family: as a result of the earthquake, construction work had stopped, and my father was unemployed.

No matter how hard we tried to save, the money my father earned from the repair jobs he found from time to time was not enough to support our household.

My father couldn't find work all winter. He would go with hope and return sad.

There were a few days left before Ramadan. My father came home happily - he had found a job. “Prepare your documents, come,” they said. He announced the news with joy and left the house at lightning speed. “I have to deliver today,” he said.

A few hours had passed when our neighbor came in with a panicked expression on his face.

“Don’t be afraid, but your father has had a minor accident,” he said.

When we arrived at the hospital, my father’s face was pale. His arms and legs were bandaged. A car speeding through a red light had hit my father. When we prayed that he was alive, my father tried to hide the tears that were flowing from his eyes, embarrassedly saying, “Why did I survive? I made your burden heavier.”

After a while, my father came home. Our stove wasn’t burning, our house was cold. Several of our neighbors had called the municipality and asked for coal for us. “No way,” they said — we had to register in advance.

And so, our “sugar-free” Ramadan holiday came and went. Despite all the difficulties, the weather had warmed up a little, the bandage on my father’s arm had come off, but his leg was still bandaged.

Then came Eid al-Adha. I thought to myself: “There are twelve apartments in our building, many of them will sacrifice. At least they will give us a share, and my mother will cook my favorite meat dishes.” As I watched from the window, the sacrificial animals were slaughtered, skinned, and bowls of meat were carried to the houses in the empty area opposite. Every time the door was opened, the delicious smell of meat being roasted in the houses spread to our house. But unfortunately, no one brought us a piece of meat.

Meanwhile, my grandmother called from the village to congratulate us on our holiday. She sensed that I was worried by the sound of my voice as she spoke. She asked insistently and anxiously: “Has your father gotten worse?”

“No,” I said slowly. “The neighbors didn’t give us sacrificial meat.”

In the summer months, we would go to the village to my grandmother. She had a cow and five or six chickens. Her cow gave good milk. When she milked it, my grandmother would sing songs to her.

On the third day of the holiday, there was a knock on our door early in the morning. It was my grandmother who came. We welcomed her with joy. She hugged us all, crying. “My lambs, my lambs, I brought you a lot of meat,” she said.

We filled the refrigerator to the brim with meat. My sister quickly chopped the meat. The delicious smell of the meat cooking further fueled my appetite, which had been growing for two days. My mouth was watering as I stood at the stove. I had no patience to wait for the table. I picked up the fork and dipped it into the meat. I was whistling as I brought the skewer to my mouth when my father’s words to my grandmother almost pierced my ears:

— Oh, mother, oh! Why did you slaughter that cow?

My heart was pounding, my head was spinning. I threw the fork in my hand and ran outside. My grandmother's hope, the cow she milked while singing songs, had been dashed because my soul craved meat?

The table was set, they pulled me by the arm and insisted on making me sit down. I reluctantly and shyly dipped my fork in again. The skewer was stuck in my throat. Tears fell from my eyes. “What happened, what's wrong with you?” they asked.

— My tooth!… — I said. — My tooth hurts a lot.

Moral: A holiday is a real holiday when it is everyone's holiday. It is humanity's duty not to forget those who do not see meat in their homes, those who are in need, in the midst of abundance.

The Mullah and the Scholar

Text

A scholar from another country comes to Tamerlane; after much talk through an interpreter, he says he wishes to meet the scholars of this land. Tamerlane gathers all the scholars. None of them dares to debate the foreigner. At last, seeing their fear, the Mullah takes the debate upon himself.

A gathering is held. The foreign scholar draws a circle on the ground and faces the Mullah. The Mullah thinks a moment and divides the circle in two. Seeing the scholar silent, he draws another line, divides it into four, pulls three parts toward himself and acts as if giving one part to the foreigner. The scholar agrees. A third time the scholar gestures as if eating. The Mullah quickly takes an egg from his pocket and flaps his arms like bird wings. The scholar laughs, kisses the Mullah’s hand, and the Mullah leaves.

Through the interpreter Tamerlane asks the scholar: “We understood nothing of these signs.”

The scholar says: “Views of the world differ. By drawing that circle I meant that the earth is round, and I wanted your scholar’s opinion. Mashallah — he quickly drew the equator, then divided the globe into four and showed that three parts are water and one is land. Then I asked about the life of plants on the globe. Your scholar answered rightly again — that all plants live by rain and the sun’s rays.”

“As a third question I wanted his view of humans and animals; with an egg he quickly reminded me that birds too belong to that kind.”

After the scholar left, Tamerlane called the Mullah and asked him about the exchange.

The Mullah said: “Ah, the scholar… I thought he was truly a deep man. Turns out he was a simple glutton… First he drew a circle on the ground and said to me, if only there were a pan of omelette here now. I quickly halved it and said they wouldn’t give it all to you — we’re here too. I saw my friend wasn’t satisfied. I quickly split it again and took three shares myself, so he’d see what greed gets you. The man saw the omelette slipping away and agreed. The second time he raised his hand to the sky as if to say: how about a pot of pilaf?

I held my hand palm-down and said: if there’s any black on top, I’ll eat it. The third time he showed me his belly and said: I’ve come from the road, I’m hungry. I quickly took the egg from my pocket and said: for your sake — I’m hungrier than you, and I’ve only one egg in my pocket; don’t insist, I can’t give it.”

Moral: The same sign is read differently in each person’s world. We often see the other in the mirror of our own thoughts.

The Price of a Miracle

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

(A True Story)

Lisa was eight years old when she heard her parents talking about her younger brother, Oliver. All the little girl knew was that her brother was very sick and that her parents had no money for his treatment.

Only a very expensive operation could save Oliver. There was no one to lend them money. Lisa heard her father whisper helplessly to her tearful mother, “Only a miracle can save him now.”

The girl went into her room and took out a glass jar that she had kept in a secret place in the closet. She poured out all the money in the jar on the floor and counted it carefully—she did this several times. When she was finished counting, she carefully put the coins in the jar, closed the lid, and went out the back door to the pharmacy.

As soon as she reached the pharmacy, she went inside and patiently waited for the pharmacist to pay attention to her. The pharmacist was busy, his mind was busy talking. Although Lisa tried to get his attention with various movements and sounds, the man remained silent. Finally, the little girl took some of her money out of the container and slammed it on the glass counter. The pharmacist was startled:

— My daughter, what do you want? — he asked nervously and, without waiting for her answer, added: — I am talking to my brother right now, whom I have not seen for a long time.

— I also want to talk to you about my brother, — Lisa answered with the same nervousness. — He is sick... I need a miracle to cure him.

— What? You want a miracle? — the pharmacist became even more irritated.

— His name is Oliver, and something bad is growing in his brain. My parents say that now only a miracle can save him. Tell me, how much does a miracle cost?

— We do not sell miracles here, my daughter. I am sorry, but I cannot help you, — the pharmacist said in a slightly soft voice.

— Look, I have money. If it is not enough, I will find the rest and bring it. Just tell me the price of a miracle.

A smartly dressed man who had been watching the conversation from the sidelines until now—the pharmacist’s brother—leaned down and asked Lisa:

— What kind of miracle does your brother need?

— I don’t know,— Lisa answered.— All I know is that he is really sick, and my parents say he needs an operation. Since my parents don’t have the money for an operation, I decided to spend my own money.

— How much money do you have?— the man asked.

— One dollar and eleven cents,— Lisa answered slowly.— That’s all I have, but I can add a little more if necessary.

— One dollar and eleven cents... What a wonderful coincidence,— the man smiled.— That’s exactly the price of the miracle your little brother needs.

The man took the money from the girl, took her hand, and said:

— Take me home. I want to see your brother, talk to your parents. Let’s see if I have the miracle your brother needs.

This handsome man was Dr. Carlton Armstrong, a renowned neurosurgeon.

The operation was free of charge, and soon Oliver was back home. Mom and Dad were overjoyed. Mom whispered to Dad, “This is a real miracle. How much did the operation cost?”

Lisa’s lips parted, and she smiled with satisfaction. Because she knew exactly how much this miracle had cost: one dollar and eleven cents—plus the little girl’s belief in the existence of a miracle that could heal her brother.

Moral: Faith is a powerful force. Sometimes, when a child’s pure faith meets the kindness of a human being, a miracle is born.

One day, a professor at a university in America addressed his students as follows while starting a class:
