Belling the Cat

Audio
Image
Text

One day, the mice gathered to discuss how to protect themselves from the cat that never left them in peace.

Everyone had a suggestion, but none seemed suitable. At last, a young mouse spoke up.
“Let’s hang a bell around the cat’s neck! Whenever it comes near, we’ll hear it and hide. It won’t be able to catch us by surprise anymore.”

The mice looked at one another in delight. What a wonderful idea! Why hadn’t they thought of it before? They all applauded the young mouse.

When the excitement had died down a little, an old mouse who had been listening quietly in a corner spoke.
“A fine idea. With a bell around its neck, the cat won’t be able to sneak up on us. But one question remains: who will put the bell on the cat?”

Everyone fell silent. Not a single mouse answered.

Moral: A good idea is only part of the task. Before applauding a plan, consider who will carry it out—and how.

Humanity

Audio
Image
Text

How full the world is of wonders and delicate beauty... In every flower, in every whisper of the wind, in the color of the sky, there is a hidden wisdom. This beauty is like a silent trust placed in the human soul. Sadly, however, people sometimes disrupt this harmony with their own hands, burning not only the land but also hopes, destinies, and memories.

War damages not only the land but also the light within us. Every life lost is, in truth, a part of the world falling silent. Yet our purpose in life is not to destroy, but to protect, create, and sustain life.

Perhaps the greatest question is this: When will humanity find its strength in sustaining life rather than destroying it? When will the earth be filled with the sound of peace rather than silence? Perhaps then the world will be not only beautiful, but also a place that is cherished.

Moral: Humanity is elevated by protecting life and serving peace and goodness. The more we devote our strength to sustaining life, the more beautiful the world becomes for us all.

Who was keeping watch?

Audio
Image
Text

Long ago, an elderly woman’s home was robbed. Distraught, she went before the king to complain.

After hearing her story, the king asked indignantly:

“Why were you sleeping so soundly that you didn’t even notice a thief entering your home?”

The woman replied:

“Your Majesty, we thought you were keeping watch. That is why we slept so peacefully at home.”

Moral: People’s peace of mind rests on their safety, not on promises. A ruler’s duty is to fulfill their responsibilities, not to blame those who have suffered.

Sharing Justice

Audio
Image
Text

Let no one shed a tear,
or let those who always smile remember those who weep.

Let there be no curse called money,
or let those with wealth learn how to share.

Let no one say “I love you,”
unless their actions show what love truly means.

Let no festival come,
unless there is meat for every table.

Moral: Justice means that everyone has a share in joy, sustenance, and love. True humanity is remembering another’s hardship even in our own happiness—and sharing what we have.

The judge's answer

Audio
Image
Text

According to an old tale, a merchant lost a purse containing 800 guilders in Frankfurt in 1506. A carpenter passing along the same road found it. An honest, devout man, he reasoned that anyone who had lost so much money would surely come looking for it. He kept the purse safe, intending to return it to its owner.

One day, after a church service, the priest made an announcement:

“A merchant visiting our city has lost 800 guilders. He promises a reward of 100 guilders to whoever returns the money.”

As soon as the carpenter heard this, he went home, fetched the purse, and handed it to the priest.

The merchant came to the church when he heard the news. He counted his money, but instead of the promised 100 guilders, he offered the carpenter just five. The carpenter asked him to honor his word.

Unwilling to pay the reward, the merchant protested:

“There were 900 guilders in my purse, not 800. You’ve already taken your hundred!”

The priest intervened. He had known the carpenter for years and vouched for his honesty. When the dispute grew heated, the matter was brought before the town court.

The judge first addressed the merchant:

“Will you place your hand on the Bible and swear that your purse contained 900 guilders?”

The merchant took the oath without hesitation.

Then the judge turned to the carpenter:

“And do you swear that the purse you found contained 800 guilders?”

The carpenter also placed his hand on the Bible and swore.

As everyone waited for the ruling, the judge said calmly:

“The matter is clear. The carpenter found 800 guilders, while the merchant has sworn that he lost 900. Therefore, the purse that was found cannot belong to this merchant. It will remain with the carpenter until its rightful owner comes forward. The merchant may continue searching for his missing 900 guilders.”

Moral: Greed can cost us what we already have while we reach for more. Those who lie to deny others their due may find that their own words expose them.

The Lambs’ Skins

Audio
Image
Text

“Better to shear the rams than skin the lambs.”

This saying appears in a tale associated with Asad Pasha al-Azm, an Ottoman governor of Damascus.

According to the tale, one day Asad Pasha discovered that the treasury was empty. He summoned his advisers to find a solution. They proposed imposing an additional tax on the city’s weavers.

“How much would that raise?” the Pasha asked.

“Fifty or sixty purses of gold.”

“But these people can barely make ends meet. How would they pay?”

“They could sell whatever gold and jewelry they have at home, Pasha.”

Asad Pasha rejected the proposal.

“I shall find the money another way.”

The next day, he sent word to the mufti, inviting him to a private meeting that night. When the religious official arrived, the Pasha said:

“Mufti, we have received information that you drink alcohol secretly in your home. That goes against both your office and the rules you uphold. I must report the matter to Istanbul, but I wished to speak to you first.”

Alarmed, the mufti began to plead. He offered a thousand gold coins to keep the news from reaching Istanbul. The Pasha refused. The mufti doubled his offer, but the Pasha refused again. Eventually, they settled on six thousand gold coins.

The following day, the Pasha summoned the judge.

“We have learned from reliable sources that you accept bribes and use your position for personal gain.”

Fearing for his office and reputation, the judge also began to plead.

“Please do not dismiss me, sir. I would be disgraced.”

After much bargaining, he too agreed to pay six thousand gold coins.

Next, the Pasha summoned the treasurer, the chief of public order, merchants, and other wealthy men, one by one. According to the tale, he collected two hundred purses of gold in this way.

Then he called his advisers together again.

“Have you heard of me imposing an additional tax on the people of Damascus?”

“No, Pasha.”

“And yet I have collected not fifty, but two hundred purses of gold.”

“How did you manage that?”

The Pasha replied:

“Better to shear the rams than skin the lambs.”

Moral: Justice means sharing burdens according to people’s means, rather than taking the last morsel from those who have little. Ill-gotten gains should be dealt with through laws that apply equally to everyone, not through secret bargains.

The stolen camel

Audio
Image
Text

A Bedouin was riding his camel across the desert when he came upon a man who could barely stand. His lips were cracked with thirst.

“Water… Please give me some water,” the stranger begged.

The Bedouin dismounted and handed him his waterskin. The man drank his fill. Then, without warning, he shoved his rescuer aside, climbed onto the camel, and rode away.

Realizing he could not catch him, the Bedouin called out:
“Take the camel! But I ask one thing: don’t tell anyone how you got it!”

Surprised, the thief stopped the camel and looked back.
“Why?”

“If people hear that you repaid kindness with betrayal, they may be afraid to offer water to someone dying of thirst. I have lost my camel. I don’t want others to lose their hope of finding help.”

Moral: When we betray someone who helps us, we hurt more than that person—we weaken other people’s faith in kindness. Value the kindness you receive, and never let someone’s ingratitude make you indifferent to others.

A market of good neighbors

Audio
Image
Text

One day, a king disguises himself and goes into the city to see how his people are faring. He walks through the market, asking about the quality of the goods, their prices and the shopkeepers’ livelihoods.

He enters the first shop on the street and greets the owner.

“I’d like half a batman (a traditional unit of weight) of butter, half a batman of honey, and some cheese.”

The shopkeeper welcomes him warmly and weighs out the butter. Unaware that his customer is the king, he says:

“Sir, I can certainly sell you the honey and cheese as well. But this butter is my first sale of the day, while my neighbor has sold nothing yet. I would be grateful if you bought the rest from the shop opposite.”

Pleased by these words, the king pays for the butter and crosses the street. There, he asks for honey and cheese.

The elderly shopkeeper weighs the honey and hands it over.

“Thank God, you have brought me my first sale of the day. May I ask a favor? Please buy your cheese from the shop next door. Its owner is still waiting for his first customer.”

The king goes to the third shop and buys the cheese. He is deeply moved by the care these shopkeepers show for one another’s livelihoods.

As he leaves the market, he says with satisfaction:

“With such a people, the world could be conquered!”

Moral: A community grows stronger when people care about their neighbors’ livelihoods as well as their own. True success lies not in leaving others behind, but in moving forward together.

Choose justice

Image
Text

Words attributed to Hacı Bektaş Veli:

Why hatred when there is love?

Why war when there is peace?

Why strife when there is brotherhood?

Why enmity when there is friendship?

Why intolerance when there is tolerance?

Why captivity when there is freedom?

Why injustice when there is justice?

Moral: Love, peace and justice are more than wishes; they are values to uphold through our everyday choices. Choosing them—and defending them through our actions—is our responsibility.
