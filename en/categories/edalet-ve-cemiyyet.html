Belling the Cat

Audio
Image
Text

A group of mice gathered to discuss how to protect themselves from a cat that had been terrorizing them.

They put forward many ideas, but could agree on none. At last, a young mouse suggested fastening a bell around the cat’s neck.

Whenever the cat approached, they would hear the bell and have time to escape. The mice greeted the plan with enthusiastic applause.

Then an old mouse, who had been listening quietly in a corner, spoke up:

“That is a clever idea, and I’m sure it would work. But there is one thing I’d like to know: who is going to put the bell on the cat?”

Moral: Making a plan is easier than carrying it out. Even the cleverest idea is of little use unless someone can put it into practice.

The Woman Whose House Was Robbed

Audio
Image
Text

Long ago, an elderly woman in a certain city had her home robbed. Distraught, she went to the king to seek justice.

After hearing her story, the king asked angrily:

“How could you sleep so soundly that you didn’t notice a thief?”

The woman replied:

“We thought you were awake and watching over us, Your Majesty. That is why we felt safe enough to sleep.”

Moral: People’s sense of security grows from trust in those responsible for protecting them. When people know their ruler is keeping watch, they can sleep peacefully in their own homes.

Sharing Fairly

Audio
Image
Text

Let no one weep alone

while others laugh without a care.

Let wealth cease to be an affliction,

or let the wealthy learn to share.

Let no one merely speak of love

without showing what that love means.

Let us not call a day a feast

while others have no meat to eat.

Moral: Humanity is created when joy, bread, and love are shared. If one side laughs and the other side cries, there is no justice there.

The Judge of Frankfurt

Audio
Image
Text

According to a story set in Frankfurt in 1506, a merchant lost a purse containing 800 guilders. A carpenter found it. Deeply religious, he kept it safe, certain that whoever had lost such a sum would come looking for it.

One day, after a church service, a monk announced that a visiting merchant had lost 800 guilders and offered a reward of 100 for their return.

The carpenter brought the purse to the monk.

When the merchant collected it, however, he offered only five guilders. The carpenter asked him to honor his promise. To avoid paying, the merchant claimed the purse had contained 900 guilders and accused the carpenter of taking 100. The monk defended the carpenter’s honesty, but the argument grew heated, so he took them both to court.

The judge asked the merchant to swear on the Bible that he had lost 900 guilders. He did so without hesitation. The carpenter then swore that the purse he found had contained only 800.

The judge announced his decision:

“The carpenter found 800 guilders. The merchant lost 900. Clearly, this cannot be the merchant’s purse. Since its owner has not been found, the carpenter may keep the 800 guilders. The merchant is free to continue searching for his 900.”

Moral: Greed traps a person. The stingy person who denies the truth eventually becomes a victim of his own lies; justice is on the side of the righteous.

The Skins of the Lambs

Audio
Image
Text

“It is better to shear the wool of rams than to skin lambs.”

This saying is associated with Azmzadeh Asad Pasha, who was the governor of Damascus province during the Ottoman period. The story goes like this:

One day, faced with an empty treasury, Asad Pasha asked his advisers what to do. They proposed an extra tax on the weavers of Damascus. “How much would it raise?” he asked.

“Fifty or sixty bags of gold.”

Asad Pasha objected: “These people are already struggling to make ends meet. How will they pay this tax?” The advisors said: “They will sell the gold and jewels in their houses, Pasha,” and tried to persuade him.

“I can raise it another way,” the pasha replied.

His advisers fell silent.

The next evening, he summoned the mufti to a private meeting.

“Mufti Effendi, I hear you have been secretly drinking at home, in defiance of religious law. I must report this to Istanbul, but thought I should tell you first.”

The mufti pleaded with him and offered a thousand mecidiyes to keep the matter quiet. The pasha refused. After further bargaining, they settled on six thousand.

Next he called in the judge. “Reliable sources tell me you accept bribes and use your office for personal gain.”

The judge begged not to be dismissed and disgraced. He too agreed to pay six thousand mecidiyes.

Then Asad Pasha invites the treasurer, the chief of security, the merchants and the wealthy to a meeting one by one.

By the end, he had collected two hundred bags of gold. He called his advisers together. “Have you heard of any new tax on the people of Damascus?” They had not.

“Yet I have raised two hundred bags, not fifty.”

“How did you do it?”

The Pasha replies: “It is better to shear the wool of rams than to skin lambs.”

Moral: It is more just to take the unjust profit of the wealthy than to rob the poor. The burden should be placed on the strong, not the poor.

The Bedouin and the Stolen Camel

Audio
Image
Text

A Bedouin riding through the desert came across a man who could barely stand, his lips parched with thirst. The man begged for water. The Bedouin dismounted and gave him a drink. As soon as he had finished, the stranger shoved the Bedouin aside, climbed onto the camel, and rode away.

Realizing that he could not recover his camel, the Bedouin shouted after the thief:

“Take the camel! It’s yours. But never tell anyone what happened here.”

Puzzled by this request, the thief stopped, looked back, and asked why.

“If you tell anyone, the story will spread,” the Bedouin replied. “Then people may stop helping strangers in need.”

Moral: Sometimes a person bears a loss quietly to protect others’ faith in human kindness. One story of betrayal can discourage countless acts of generosity.

The Strength of a Generous People

Audio
Image
Text

A king went into the city in disguise to see how his people lived. He walked through the markets, observing the goods, prices, and shopkeepers.

The king entered the first shop at the beginning of the street. He greeted and said:

“Half a batman of butter, half a batman of honey, and some cheese, please.” The batman was a traditional measure of weight.

The shopkeeper greeted him warmly and weighed out the butter.

“I can sell you the honey and cheese too,” he said, “but this is my first sale today. My neighbor has not yet had one. Would you buy the rest from him?”

Pleased, the king paid and crossed the street. There he asked for the honey and cheese. The second shopkeeper weighed the honey, then said:

“Thank Allah, I’ve made my first sale. Would you buy your cheese next door? That shopkeeper is still waiting for his.”

The king, who was very impressed by what he saw, went to the shop next door and made his purchase, and said:

“With people of such character, one could conquer the world,” he said, leaving the market delighted.

Moral: A people’s strength rests in honesty and generosity. Winning hearts is a greater victory than winning territory.

Why enmity when there is friendship?
