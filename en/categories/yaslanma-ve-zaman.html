Life After Sixty

Audio
Image
Text

It is time to use the money you have saved up so far. Use it and enjoy it. Don’t save it for those who know nothing of the sacrifices you made to earn it. This is the ideal time to enjoy peace.

Do not worry about the financial situation of your children and grandchildren, and do not be upset that you have spent your money on yourself. You have taken care of them for years, taught them everything you could. You have given them education, food, shelter and support. Now it is their duty to earn their own money. Let them find their own way.

Take care of your health without overexerting yourself. Exercise moderately, walk each day, eat well, and get enough sleep.

At this age, it is easy to get sick and difficult to stay healthy. Therefore, you should keep yourself in good shape, pay attention to your medical and physical needs. Contact your doctor; get check-ups even if you feel fine.

Do not let small troubles consume you. You have already faced much in life and carry both happy and painful memories. Do not let the past hold you back or the future frighten you. Enjoy the present; many everyday worries will soon be forgotten.

Whatever your age, keep loving your partner, your family, and life itself. A lively mind and a compassionate heart can keep you young in spirit.

Keep caring for your appearance and well-being. Visit the hairdresser, care for your nails and skin, and look after your teeth. Feeling well groomed can lift your spirits and strengthen your confidence.

Don't lose sight of age-appropriate fashion trends, but maintain your own style. Read books, flip through newspapers, watch the news. Go online, find out what people think. Make sure you have an active email account and use at least a few social networks.

Respect younger people and their opinions. They may disagree with you, but they must find their own way. Offer advice rather than criticism, and share the wisdom of your experience.

Never use the phrase "in our time...". Now is your time. Be a part of this time as long as you live.

Spend less time with people who leave you feeling discouraged and more with those whose company brings you joy.

Do not give up your hobbies. You can travel, walk, cook, read, dance. If you want, you can keep a cat or a dog.

Get out of the house, meet people you have not seen for a long time, and either experience something new or relive something from the past. The main thing is to get out of the house from time to time. Go to museums, walk in the park.

Try to always be polite, do not complain and criticize unless necessary. If someone has broken your heart, forgive them. Apologize to those whose hearts you have broken.

Don't carry your pain with you. It will only hurt you. It doesn't matter who is right. They say that holding a grudge is like poisoning yourself and waiting for someone else to die. Never accept that poison. Forgive, forget and move on with your life.

Laugh, enjoy yourself, and welcome the peace this stage of life can offer.

Moral: After years of caring for others, allow yourself to enjoy life too. Your happiness can brighten the lives of those around you.

What Changed After Sixty

Audio
Image
Text

I asked a friend nearing seventy what age had changed in him. He replied:

“I no longer tell older people, ‘You’ve already told me that story.’ Telling it again lets them revisit a happy memory.

“I no longer feel I must correct every mistake. It is not my job to make everyone perfect. Being there for someone matters more.

“I give more compliments. They brighten my day as well as the other person’s.

“I worry less about a crease or a stain on my clothes. My worth does not lie in my appearance.

“I have learned to step away from relationships in which we cannot understand or treat each other well.

“I am no longer ashamed of my feelings. They are part of being human.

“I would rather set pride aside than destroy a relationship. Pride can leave you alone.

“I make time for things that bring me joy. My happiness is partly my responsibility.

“I have learned to ask before helping. Someone may need support without wanting the kind I offer.

“I try not to spend my days complaining about health, neighbors, relatives, or retirement. Despite the difficulties, I am alive and still have people I love.

“I have stopped saying, ‘When I was your age,’ or ‘I gave you my best years.’ Each generation faces its own circumstances.

“I pay more attention to my wife. I remember the lively young woman she was and recognize how much she needs my care now.

“I no longer blame myself for everything. I have done what I could for myself and my children.

“And I ask the Lord for strength to change what I can, patience to accept what I cannot, and wisdom to know the difference.”

Moral: Age teaches a person the greatest wisdom: peace is better than perfection, and understanding is better than being right.
