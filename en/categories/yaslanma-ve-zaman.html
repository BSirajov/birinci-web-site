Time — life flowing silently

Audio
Image
Text

Time is a strange wonder... We can neither see it, nor hold it, nor bring it back. It passes silently—in the hands of clocks, in the changing days, in the gray appearing in our hair, in children growing up, in memories receding into the distance...

Often, we understand time only after we have lost it. A day that seemed ordinary yesterday becomes our most treasured memory years later. A conversation, a glance, a smile that we once considered insignificant, or a simple evening spent with someone we love, may later become one of the most beautiful moments of our lives.

Time stops for no one. It yields neither to wealth, nor to fame, nor to human desire. It is the greatest treasure, given to everyone in equal measure, yet valued differently by each of us. A person's life is measured, in truth, not in years but in the meaningful moments they have lived.

Sometimes we think that life still lies ahead and that we have plenty of time. We will do it tomorrow, go next week, talk someday, meet later... But the time we call ‘later’ does not always come.

Time takes much from us: our youth, certain people, certain dreams, certain opportunities... Yet in return it gives us memories, experience, and the chance to understand the true meaning of life.

So let us not measure time simply by the years that pass. Let us value our time not by how long we live, but by how we live. Let us tell our loved ones today that we love them. Let us not put off meeting our friends. Let us look at beautiful landscapes, watch the sunrise, listen to the sea, and live the moments that bring joy to our hearts.

For time passes...

Moral: Let us not put off expressing our love or making time for our loved ones. A moment that has passed does not return; we must appreciate this day today.

Etibar Siracsoy

To Those Who Have Reached Sixty

Audio
Image
Text

It is time to use the money you have saved up so far. Use it and enjoy it. Don’t deprive yourself of everything just to keep it for those who know nothing of the labor you spent to earn it. This is the ideal time to enjoy peace.

Do not worry about the financial situation of your children and grandchildren, and do not be upset that you have spent your money on yourself. You have taken care of them for years, taught them everything you could. You have given them education, food, shelter and support. Now it is their duty to earn their own money. Let them find their own way.

Take care of your health without overexerting yourself. Exercise moderately, walk each day, eat well, and get enough sleep.

At this age, it is easy to get sick and difficult to stay healthy. Therefore, you should keep yourself in good shape, pay attention to your medical and physical needs. Contact your doctor; get check-ups even if you feel fine.

Do not let small troubles consume you. You have already overcome many difficulties and carry both happy and painful memories. Do not let the past hold you back or the future frighten you. Enjoy the present; many everyday worries will soon be forgotten.

Whatever your age, keep loving your partner, your family, and life itself. As long as your mind is sound and your compassion is intact, you are not old.

Keep caring for your appearance and well-being. Go to the hairdresser, care for your nails, see a dermatologist and a dentist, and use perfume and creams. A neat appearance will also lift your mood, and you will feel more confident and more vigorous.

Don't lose sight of age-appropriate fashion trends, but maintain your own style. Read books, flip through newspapers, watch the news. Go online, find out what people think. Make sure you have an active email account and use at least a few social networks.

Respect younger people and their opinions. They may not share your opinion, but the future is theirs, and they will steer their lives as they wish. Do not criticize; give advice, and try to remind them that yesterday's wisdom is still valid today.

Never use the phrase "in our time...". Now is your time. Be a part of this time as long as you live.

Try to stay away from negative people and spend your time only with those who give you a pleasant mood and positive energy.

Do not give up your hobbies. You can travel, walk, cook, read, dance. If you want, you can keep a cat or a dog.

Get out of the house, meet people you have not seen for a long time, and see new places and renew old memories. The main thing is not to shut yourself in at home. Go to museums, walk in the park.

Try to always be polite, do not complain and criticize unless necessary. If someone has broken your heart, forgive them. Apologize to those whose hearts you have broken.

Don't carry your pain with you. It will only hurt you. It doesn't matter who is right. They say that holding a grudge is like poisoning yourself and waiting for someone else to die. Never accept that poison. Forgive, forget and move on with your life.

Laugh, rejoice. At this stage of your life, savor a peaceful life... Be happy!

Moral: The season of sacrifice ends one day; in the autumn of life, give yourself the right to live as well. A happy person also spreads happiness around them.

What Changed After Sixty

Audio
Image
Text

I asked a friend nearing seventy what changes she had noticed in herself as she grew older. She replied:

“I no longer tell older people, ‘You’ve already told me that story.’ Telling it again lets them revisit a happy memory.

“I no longer feel I must correct every mistake. It is not my job to make everyone perfect. Peace of heart matters more than perfection.

“I give more compliments. They brighten my day as well as the other person’s.

“I worry less about a crease or a stain on my clothes. A person's worth comes not from outward appearance, but from within.

“I have learned to stay away from people who are alien to my world. They cannot build or keep a good relationship; I, for my part, have learned to do that too.

“I am no longer ashamed of my feelings. It is my own feelings that make me human.

“I would rather set pride aside than destroy a relationship. Undue pride leaves a person alone, while good relationships protect against loneliness.

“I make time for things that bring me joy. I myself am responsible for my own happiness.

“I have learned to ask before helping. Someone who needs help may not want that help to come from me.

“I try not to spend my days complaining about health, neighbors, relatives, or retirement. Despite all the difficulties, I should be grateful that I am alive and that my loved ones are beside me.

“I have stopped saying, ‘When I was your age,’ or ‘I gave you my best years.’ Each age has its own conditions, and today cannot be measured by the standard of the past.

“I pay more attention to my spouse. I remember that he was once young, strong, and full of joy, even if he is now a wrinkled, helpless, and sorrowful old man. Perhaps he is the only person who truly needs me now.

“I no longer blame myself for everything. I have done what I could for myself and my children.

“And finally I learned to ask the Lord for strength for what I can change, patience for what I cannot change, and wisdom to tell the two apart. As a human being, I am not capable of everything.”

Moral: Age teaches a person the greatest wisdom: peace is better than perfection, and understanding is better than being right.
