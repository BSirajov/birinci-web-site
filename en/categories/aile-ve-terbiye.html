A Mother’s Love

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

Erich Fromm

I am one year old—completely happy, because my mother is always beside me.

I am two—my mother is with me only sometimes. I wonder what is happening.

I am three—my mother is at work, and my nanny is with me.

I am five—my mother is in a meeting, and my grandmother is with me.

I am seven—my mother is at a seminar; I am at school with my teacher.

I am thirteen—a teenager. My mother still has to work, and my friends are beside me.

I am seventeen—my mother comes home exhausted. I am at school, in class, or at a café with friends.

I am twenty—young and confident. My mother is not at home, but my friends are with me.

I am twenty-three—I have started working. My mother is home now, while I am at work.

I am thirty—and I wonder: when did my mother and I last spend real time together? Something is wrong.

I am thirty-five—married. My mother lives in her home, and I in mine.

I am forty—my mother waits for me just as I once waited for her as a child.

I am fifty—I have grown older, yet one wish never ages: to press my heart to hers, embrace her, and rest my head on her tired knees.

I am fifty-five—another mother is always beside me now, the mother of my children. My own mother has passed away, and I live with the ache of missing her.

In the rush of ordinary life, one of our gravest failures is not giving enough time to our children and those we love. Children are the most precious characters in the extraordinary film of life unfolding before us. If we fail to notice them growing, those passing moments can never be recovered.

Life has no stop, play, forward, or rewind buttons that can show us its vanished scenes again. May we understand this before it is too late.

Love your child today. Hold them, take their hand, and remain beside them. Do not miss today’s episode, because tomorrow a new scene of childhood will already have begun.

“A mother’s love is a selfless, protective, and warm refuge. Precisely because it is unconditional, it cannot be obtained by force.”

Moral: A mother’s love is unconditional and irreplaceable. Make time for those you love today, because time is the most precious thing we can never recover.

Fakir Baykurt's unforgettable mother's advice

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

One day, Fakir Baykurt (Turkish writer, 1929-1999) asked his mother for tea: "Mom, my heart wants tea, come and have some tea," he said. The mother did not let her son down and took him to Topal Hüseyin's nearby teahouse and called out, "Master, please bring a cup of tea for my child."

After the tea arrived, Fakir Baykurt, who was so excited at that moment and did not know how to drink the tea, took a big sip of the hot tea and suddenly threw the cup on the ground as it was near his mouth.

Although the tea cup did not break because it fell to the ground, Fakir Baykurt thought that his mother would slap him. But that did not happen. His mother called Topal Hüseyin and asked him to bring another cup of tea.

Baykurt drank the second cup of tea this time in a slurping motion. For years, he asked his mother why she didn't slap him that day, but his mother left this question unanswered every time...

Fakir Baykurt's mother answered this question years later at the village school where her son taught. Fakir Baykurt remembers that day when his mother, Elif Khanum, attended his son's lesson:

— I got angry, excited, and roared during class! When the lesson was over, I asked my mother outside: "Mom, how was it, did you like my teaching?"

And my mother said: "Oh, what can I say, son, it wasn't bad."

— Mom, the inspectors come, they like it, and they give good grades. And you say "it wasn't bad." How can this be? — I asked.

And my mother said these words that can be a lesson to everyone: "For years, you asked me why I didn't slap you. Now I'm telling you, open your ears and listen!

If I had been angry the day you poured tea on me, the lion inside would have raged. If I had beaten him, that lion would have died! Then you wouldn't be a teacher like that. You should act like me, be calm. Don't try to kill the lion inside these children with a stick."

Moral: Don't kill the "lion" inside a child — his self-confidence — by beating him. True education is not given with fear, but with patience and love.

Father and son

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

The son had left home after a heated argument with his father. The father, who was heartbroken, was also angry and forbade his son's name to be mentioned in the house. The mother, however, would sit on her son's bed every night and go to sleep sniffing his pillow.

— I miss my son. What can I do, let's go find him and bring him home, — she said, but the father did not agree.

A long time had passed.

One day, seeing that the mother was constantly in tears, the father felt sorry for her; he took a piece of paper with his son's address written on it from his pocket and handed it to the mother:

— Go, you can find him at this address, — he said. — But never tell him that I gave him the address.

Although the father said a few more words, the mother did not seem to hear him; all that was left in her mind was the address. She was overjoyed. She immediately got ready, set off and reached the address written on the paper. It was a repair shop. Her son was working in overalls. The mother stared at her son with tearful eyes for a few minutes, then finally walked towards him. The son, who had been unaware of his family for a long time, thinking that everyone had forgotten about him, was at first surprised when he saw his mother, then hugged her tightly.

He asked everyone except his father, asked how he was, and finally:

— How is that man, is he still angry? — he asked his mother.

The mother left this question unanswered:

— Come on, son, let's go home, — he said.

— No, mother, I won't go. I can't live in the same house with that man again, — he said and started walking towards work.

The mother, who had been watching her son for a while, called out to give him the sweets she had prepared.

The son took the gift:

— What's wrong, mother, don't insist, I won't come. I can't live in the same house with a man who hasn't even looked for me once, isn't interested in me, — he said.

The mother, with her neck bent, said:

— Well, son, it's best that you know. I see that your decision is firm, you will not return home. But your father ordered you to stay away from the person you have been friends with for the past month - he will not do you any good. Let him reconcile with his old friend, - he said.

Hearing this, the son froze in amazement.

The mother returned home and rebuked the father:

- Man, if you knew where he was, why did you hide it from me? So, that's why you were so relaxed?

The father, who always seemed nervous, broke down, tears flowed from his eyes:

- He is my soul, my soul, - he said.

- How long have you known? - the mother asked.

- Since he left. Sometimes during the lunch break, I would go and watch from afar to see what he was eating and drinking. This was also the reason why I sometimes came home late: I would go and watch our son in the evenings to see what he was doing, who he was sitting with, - the father replied.

While the elders were hugging each other and crying, there was a knock on the door. The mother wiped away her tears and went to open the door.

The son had returned home. He kissed his parents' hands and hugged them. He had understood - the father he thought had forgotten him, torn him from his heart, had actually never taken his eyes off his son.

Moral: Don't hide your love, show it. Sometimes a timely step can prevent years of resentment.

From the wise father's advice to his son

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

O my beloved son!

Every human being should have a mind that can help them to establish different relationships with others. This mind is the inexhaustible source of all happiness and joy.

Life cannot be satisfied; its short or long life does not diminish its value.

Fruit only falls beautifully from the branch when it is ripe. Similarly, people have always preferred those who have seen the light and experienced.

The son of man usually commits the wrongdoings he commits in his youth without thinking about it. But when he remembers them later, these sins pierce his heart like a dagger and torment him.

Those who follow the right and good path in their youth will be respected and honored in their old age in return.

It is good to be strong and powerful. However, strength must be given to the command of the mind.

The Almighty Allah, the creator of the worlds, did not send the son of man to this world to live forever, but wanted him to taste death at the end of his life.

Know this well: Allah Almighty has thoroughly tested and accustomed me to two things that have kept me healthy up to this age. One of them is to eat little, and the other is to walk around to digest what I eat.

I also want you to know this: there are three types of people in this world:

The first is those who have their minds and thoughts in order, and who can see and think more or less about the future;

The second is those who are far from knowing whether their path is right or wrong, but who fall into this state not of their own will, but due to the influence of those around them. When they are advised, they accept and listen to words.

The third is those who neither know anything themselves, nor listen to the advice given, but think they know everything. These are the most miserable of all.

O son!

If Allah Almighty has created you from the people I have listed in the first place, I will be happy.

If you are from the second, I advise you to listen to the advice given to you.

Definitely do not be from the third. They are in no good standing with Allah or man.

Moral: Wisdom is the source of happiness, and strength is the servant of wisdom. He who listens to advice will be exalted; but he who thinks he knows everything will perish.

Grandfather and Grandson at the Market

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

A grandfather comes to the supermarket with his grandson. They walk the aisles, choose groceries, and fill the basket. Meanwhile the grandson keeps shouting, “Buy me this, buy me that!” and turns the shop upside down. Each time the grandfather says, “Be quiet, Arif — we’ll go home when we finish shopping.” The child reaches the limit of his frustration, takes a Kinder Surprise from the shelf, puts it under his foot, and crushes it. Again the grandfather says, “Be quiet, Arif — we’ll go home when we finish shopping,” then, crestfallen, turns to a nearby worker: “I’m so sorry — I’ll pay for this too.”

At last they pay at the checkout and leave. A woman who has watched the whole scene follows them, approaches the grandfather, and says: “I was impressed by how you behaved with Arif. You are a very patient man. Well done!” The old man thanks her and smiles: “The child’s name is Rafiq — I am Arif.”

Moral: Being able to control yourself in a tense moment is a sign of true strength. Keeping calm sometimes means mastering yourself, not others.

Hold my hand

Audio

Text

A little girl and her father are crossing a bridge. The father, a little worried, asks his daughter to hold his hand:

— My sweet daughter, hold my hand tightly so that you don't fall into the river.

The little girl replies:

— No, father, you hold my hand.

The father, a little confused, asks curiously:

— What's the difference?

— It's a big difference, — the little girl replies. — If I hold your hand and something happens to me, I might let go of it. But if you hold my hand, I'm sure that no matter what happens, you won't let go of my hand.

Moral: The power of love is not letting go. Instead of waiting for the hand of those who love you to hold theirs, hold theirs tightly.

Mother and son

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

The man put the suitcases he was carrying against the wall. Then he stood up, took a deep breath, and said to his mother, “Mom, this is a very beautiful place.” “Of course, it is beautiful, my son,” the old woman replied. For a while, they were both silent, carefully examining the room.

Then the woman opened her small suitcase and took out her nightgown, slippers, a few towels, and a framed picture. She placed the picture on the table next to her bed.

A boy in a school uniform, with a soldierly posture, was looking out of the picture. A child with full eyes and pursed lips...

The man approached the window and opened the curtain. A pale daylight filled the room. It was freezing outside. He leaned his hands on the heater and watched the muddy cars passing by for a while. Then, without even turning his head, he asked, “Is this closet enough for you, Mom?”

The old woman was lost in thought, so she didn’t even hear her son. She was leaning slightly to one side on her bed, her eyes fixed on the boy in the worn frame, his eyes fixed on the boy with a frightened look.

It was a picture they had taken on the first day of school.

The woman suddenly went back years ago. The day before, she had spent hours explaining how wonderful school was, but she had barely managed to get her son into the classroom. He had been at school all day, and every break she had gone to him and said, “I’m here, son, I’m waiting for you.” But the boy had clung to his mother’s hand even tighter and cried, “Don’t leave me, mom, I’m so scared.”

The sun was setting. The man took his hands off the heater and leaned them against the window, sweating. “Open your other suitcase too, let’s put your things in it, mom,” he said.

“Don’t delay, son, I’ll put them in myself. I don’t have much stuff anyway,” the old woman said. She reached out her trembling hands and took her son’s hand. She seemed to be about to say something, but then she stopped. She swallowed: “The children will be worried too.”

The door slammed shut. The old woman was left alone in the room with the peeling paint and the soldier-like look in the picture with her son. After sitting on the bed for a while, she put on her slippers and went to the window with the curtains open. Her son was getting into the car.

The old woman was afraid. Despite the fact that she had listened for hours the day before to how beautiful this place was, she was still afraid.

Her son's handprint was still on the window glass. She carefully rested her cheek against the mark on the glass. Her eyes were full, her lips were pursed.

“Don't leave me, son, I'm so scared,” she whispered.

The words seemed to melt on the sweaty glass and flowed towards her son, who was sitting outside in his car, waving at her.

Those who lived on the same floor as the old woman heard a sobbing sound that night. The only thing they were used to in the nursing home was the sound of sobbing that echoed through the corridors at night.

Moral: Life is a circle: a child who holds onto his mother's hand one day may leave her the next. True loyalty to a parent is not physical care, but love and attention.

Parenting

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

In a family, there was a boy who did not listen to his parents. Whatever they said, the boy did the opposite. The boy did poorly at school. He neither studied hard nor completed his homework. Even though his mother got angry and beat him, it did not help. Even though his father locked him in his room and punished him, he could not cope with the child.

One day, when the mother, who was left helpless, took the child to school, she came across a “bear trainer” on the way. The trainer, surrounded by people around, was playing a tambourine, while the bear with a ring on its nose was jumping and playing.

The woman stopped and thought. She said to herself: “I can’t raise this child, but look how this man raised his bear!”

Then she approached the bear trainer and asked: — I can’t raise my child, how do you raise this wild animal?

The man smiled: — There is a way to everything. I also have my own way of raising him.

The woman said: — Please help me.

The bear trainer looked at the boy and said: — There is no difficult task. Entrust him to me for a week, I will make him obedient, — he said.

The woman was happy and started taking the boy to the bear trainer every day.

Although only a few days had passed, the boy's behavior slowly began to improve. He no longer protested against his mother. After a week, the boy became exactly as the woman wanted. The woman was very happy. She expressed her approval to the bear trainer and prayed for him many times.

The neighbors were very surprised by this. That disobedient, rebellious boy was gone, and in his place was a calm, peaceful, well-mannered boy.

The teacher at school was also pleased with this change in the boy and congratulated the mother. However, the change did not last long. Although he was not as ambitious and angry as before, this time the boy began to steal his classmates' things, do secret things and lie openly. The teacher called the boy's mother and said: “I don't know what you are going to do, but your boy has become very strange. He started stealing and lying.” The woman was very sad. Although she went to look for the bear trainer in despair, she could not find him. The man had left.

Seeing the parents’ helpless state, her wise neighbors said: “Don’t worry, I know a wise man with a big heart. If you want, go to him and explain the situation.”

The woman agreed. She took her child and took him to this wise man. After the man got acquainted with the situation, he said: — Give me this beautiful young man for a week, I will see what I can do. — The woman accepted the offer.

He started taking the child to this wise man.

After only two meetings, the man became very upset. He said to the child’s mother: — What have you done to this child? I see animal traits in him. — The woman was surprised: — I didn’t do anything, I just used to take him to the bear trainer because the child was very naughty.

The man became very angry: — Do you think an animal trainer can educate a child? Animals are educated with fear, and people with conscience. Animals are sometimes disciplined by beating, cursing, and punishing; if that doesn’t help, they are put in a dark room and left helpless. How can you entrust your child to an animal trainer?

Then he continued: — Remember that education is not about going after a child, but about entering his heart and influencing him with compassion. Take your child and go. Be close to him, kiss and hug him, listen to him, do not withhold your love from him. The more love you show your child, the more he will soften, he will see that he has parents who love him, his trust and love for you will increase.

— Your child’s cure is neither the bear trainer nor me; only you — the parents, — the wise man concluded.

Moral: A child is educated not with fear, but with love. It is not a teacher or an outsider who educates a child—it is a loving parent.

Puppies for sale

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

A little boy entered the shop after seeing a sign in the window saying “We have puppies for sale.”

“How much do you sell puppies for?” he asked.

“Between thirty and fifty dollars. Each one has a different price,” said the shopkeeper.

The little boy reached into his pocket, took out all the money he had, and placed it in front of the shopkeeper, then counted it all carefully. Finally, in a disappointed voice, he said,

“All I have is thirteen dollars and twenty-seven cents,” he said. Then, taking a deep breath, he asked,

“Can I at least see the puppies?”

The shopkeeper smiled and called to his lady in the back room to bring the puppies. A moment later, the lady came out of the room, followed by five fluffy, plump puppies. However, one of the puppies was walking with great difficulty. The boy looked at the puppy:

“What’s wrong with him? Why can’t he walk?” he asked.

“Our vet said that his tibia was congenitally deformed. He will never be able to walk comfortably, - the shopkeeper replied”.

Hearing this, the boy was excited:

“I want to buy this puppy,” the shopkeeper said:

“I don't think you want to buy it, my son. If you want, I'll give it to you.”

The little boy was very angry. He looked the shopkeeper straight in the eye and said:

“I don't want you to give him to me. That puppy is as valuable as any other. I can give you thirteen dollars and twenty-seven cents now, but I'll bring you five dollars every week until I get thirty dollars,” he said.

The shopkeeper said:

“You really don't want that puppy, my son. He won't be able to run and play with you like the others,” the little boy said:

“But we have to give him a chance, he's as valuable as any puppy,” he rebelled. Then he bent down and slowly pulled up the hem of his trousers and showed his leg, which was injured by polio, and the iron supports on it. Then he turned to the man and said in a soft voice:

— Uncle, I can't run and play either. This little one and I need friends who will understand and support us.

Moral: Every living thing needs a friend who will understand and support it. The one who is different is just as valuable as the others; we need to give him a chance.

Rotten Seed

Text

A father who called his little son, who was constantly suffering from illnesses after his wife died, despised him and gave him to an orphanage.

Years passed.

One day, this man saw his grown son in front of a school. He could not believe his eyes. The father, who looked at his son's shining eyes and healthy body with regret, said with excitement:

— I did not believe that you would recover, that you would become a man. You have become a strong warrior. How did this miracle happen? How did you get rid of your illnesses? — he asked his son.

The boy looks at his father, who once despised him, with his moist eyes, and points to the old woman who has adopted him, who is approaching him from the opposite side, smiling and saying:

— After you said, “Rotten seeds do not germinate” and gave me to the orphanage, this woman took me in as her son and never left her compassionate hands on me, saying, “Every seed watered with love will germinate.”

Moral: Words either build or destroy. It is possible to elevate or destroy someone with a single word—every seed watered with love will germinate.

Spinach

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

We lived in a remote village. We didn’t even have a proper road to get around. My mother was addicted to spinach. After my father’s death, I suspected that she had lost her mind when I was a child...

“You need to eat a lot, it gives strength to the body,” my mother would say, and for breakfast, lunch, and dinner, she would only feed us spinach, with a little cornbread on the side. I hated spinach already, I couldn’t stand to see this vegetable.

One day I told my mother: “When I grow up and have a job and start a family, I will never buy spinach at home. I won’t cook spinach at home.” She promised that she was looking forward to those days, and that she would even play at my wedding, no matter how old she was.

The day came, I moved from our village to the city to study in high school. I somehow managed to make ends meet with the small scholarship I received. Once a month, when I came home, I didn't want to eat a spoonful of the spinach dish my mother put on the table. I only wanted to go back to the dormitory and eat delicious food.

I studied. By the time I graduated from university, my master's degree, and my doctorate with government loans, my mother was already a bent old woman. When I came to the village and got married to the girl I was going to marry, my old mother both cried and played at my wedding, as she had promised years ago. At that time, I couldn't understand why my mother shed tears when she played.

Years passed. My mother passed away two years ago.

One day, I found the notebook my mother had kept at that time in an old chest. On one page of the notebook, my mother had written: “I only had spinach seeds left. I constantly planted spinach and a little corn. I didn't have any on hand or in my pocket. I cooked this dish so that my children wouldn't go hungry. But I didn't rebel. ”Spinach is very useful, so I give it to you three times a day,“ my heart ached when I said. This was all I could do for my children. I love them with all my heart.”

I loved eating spinach at the age of fifty. My wife asks me in surprise why I buy spinach so often, she thinks I love spinach. But she doesn't know that every time I smell that food, I remember the sacrifices my mother made to raise me, how she played at my wedding while crying...

Moral: We often understand a mother's sacrifice in the face of impossibility years later. Love is sometimes hidden in a plate of simple food.

Teeth falling out

Audio
Audio
Image
Text

A king saw in a dream that all his teeth had fallen out, so he called a dream interpreter and asked him what it meant. The man replied: “May the king live, this means that all your relatives will die, and you will be the last to die.” The king was angry and dismissed the man, called another and asked him the same question, and received the answer: “May the king live, you will be the longest-lived in your family.” The king was very pleased with this answer and rewarded the man and sent him on his way.

The Value of Your Family

Text

One evening, a son came to his father and said he wanted to ask him a question. The father agreed. The son got straight to the point and asked his father how much money he earned in an hour of work. The father was troubled by this question and wanted to know why his son was asking such a question. The son said he was just curious and waited for an answer. Finally, the father gave in and said that he earned a hundred dollars an hour.

Hearing this, the son lowered his head, was silent for a moment, and then asked his father to borrow fifty dollars. This request angered the father; he refused, thinking that his son would spend the money on a meaningless toy or something unnecessary, and told him to go to his room and sleep. The son went to his room without protesting, quietly closing the door.

The father tried to calm down by pacing the room. After a while, he calmed down and admitted to himself that it was wrong for his son to get angry without asking why he wanted the money. The father, tormented by a guilty conscience, went to his son's room, knocked lightly on the door, and asked if he was asleep. The son replied that he was still awake.

The father entered the room and apologized for his harsh behavior. He admitted that he had taken the stress of the day out of his son, handed him fifty dollars, and told him to spend it as he pleased. The son thanked him joyfully, took out the money he had hidden under his pillow, counted it, and looked at his father. Seeing this, the father became angry again and asked his son why he had asked for more money when he had money.

The son explained that the money he had saved before was not enough to buy what he wanted, and now he had exactly one hundred dollars. Then he asked his father if he could "buy" a watch for him - he asked him to come home from work early tomorrow and have lunch with him.

These words deeply moved the father, and his eyes filled with tears. He hugged his little son and apologized to him.

Moral: The greatest need of children is the attention, love, and time spent with them by their parents.

Text

On a cold winter evening, an elderly couple walks into McDonald's and sits down at a table. The man goes to the cashier and buys a hamburger, a large box of fries, and a large Coca-Cola. He cuts the hamburger in half and puts half in front of his wife, counts the fries one by one, and gives her half. After a while, he opens the Coca-Cola bottle, takes a sip first for himself, and then for his wife.

The people around them watch them, saying, "Two people share a meal for one, poor things."

After a while, they see that the hamburger and fries in front of the wife are still the same. The wife watches her husband eat with appetite, occasionally taking a sip of the Coke...

Finally, one of the young employees loses patience and approaches the couple and asks the man:

— Uncle, excuse me, I've been watching you for a while: you're eating, and your wife isn't touching the food. If he doesn't like it, let me bring him something else...

The old man:

— Thank you, young man, but we are happy with our situation... We have been married for sixty years and we share everything like this, — he says.

Not satisfied with this answer, the young man turns to the woman this time:

— Auntie, why don't you eat your hamburger and fries, what are you waiting for?

The old woman points to her husband and replies:

— Teeth...! I'm waiting for teeth, baby.

Moral: No one should be left in need of the one they love. May Allah not leave anyone helpless, even in their own family.

When we were in school, we would spend our summer holidays in our garden with our family. My late father wanted to get us used to hard work. One day, my father called us to him and explained: "If you want to destroy a weed, hit it deeply with the harrow; the work is not finished until you pull out its roots."

Moral: If you do not uproot both the problem and the bad habit, the appearance of cleanliness is deceptive - after a while it will reappear.
