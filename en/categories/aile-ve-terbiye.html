A Mother’s Love

Audio
Image
Text

I am one—completely happy. My mother is always beside me.

I am two—she is no longer beside me all the time. What has changed?

I am three—my mother is at work, and my nanny is with me.

I am five—my mother is in a meeting, and I am with my grandmother.

I am seven—my mother is at a seminar, and I am at school. My teacher is with me.

I am thirteen—a teenager. My mother still needs to work. I spend more and more time with my friends.

I am seventeen—my mother comes home tired from work. I am either in class or at a café with friends.

I am twenty—young, caught up in life. When my mother is not at home, my friends are there.

I am twenty-three—I have started working. Now my mother is at home, while I am at work.

I am thirty—and I wonder: when did my mother and I last sit down for an unhurried conversation? When did we last enjoy a day together? Somewhere along the way, I have missed something…

I am thirty-five—married. My mother has her home, and I have mine.

I am forty—my mother waits for me to visit, just as I once waited for her to come home.

I am fifty—I, too, am growing older. But one wish has never aged: to embrace my mother, rest my head in her lap, and be a child again beside her.

I am fifty-five—the mother of my children is beside me. My own mother is no longer alive. I live with the ache of missing her.

Amid everyday demands, we sometimes put off spending time with those dearest to us. There is always more work to do. Visits are postponed; conversations are left unfinished. Meanwhile, children grow up and parents grow older.

Life is like a film unfolding before our eyes. But we cannot rewind its scenes or pause a moment and live it again.

Hold your child today. Take their hand and listen. Make time for your parents today. Do not put today’s closeness off until tomorrow: tomorrow will be a different day.

To paraphrase Erich Fromm’s view in The Art of Loving, motherly love offers unconditional acceptance and shelter; it cannot be forced into existence or obtained by demanding it.

Moral: Love is felt not only in what we hold in our hearts, but in the time and attention we give. Tomorrow cannot bring back the day we could have shared with someone we love.

A mother's lasting lesson

Audio
Image
Text

An account of the Turkish writer Fakir Baykurt’s childhood tells how he once said to his mother:

“Mom, I’d love some tea. Shall we have a cup?”

His mother agreed and took him to a nearby teahouse run by Hüseyin, who was known for his limp.

“Please bring my boy a glass of tea,” she asked.

When the tea arrived, the boy eagerly picked up the glass and took a large gulp. It burned his mouth, and he dropped the glass. It landed on the dirt floor without breaking.

He looked anxiously at his mother, expecting a slap. Instead, she calmly called the owner and ordered another glass of tea.

This time, the boy blew on his tea and drank it in small sips.

Years passed, but Baykurt never forgot that day. Whenever he asked his mother why she had neither scolded nor slapped him, she left the question unanswered.

He finally received his answer years later, at the village school where he taught.

One day, his mother, Elif, sat in on a lesson. During the class, Baykurt lost his temper, raised his voice, and scolded the children harshly. Afterwards, he asked:

“Well, Mom? Did you like my teaching?”

“What can I say, son? It wasn’t bad.”

Her answer disappointed him.

“School inspectors observe my lessons and give me high marks. And all you can say is ‘It wasn’t bad’?”

His mother turned to him.

“For years, you’ve asked why I didn’t slap you when you spilled that tea. Now I’ll tell you. Listen carefully.”

She paused, then continued:

“If I had lost my temper with you that day, the lion inside you would have lost heart. If I had beaten you, that lion would have died. Perhaps you would never have become the teacher you are today. Be patient, son. Don’t kill the lions inside these children with shouting and blows.”

Moral: The way we respond to a child’s mistake can strengthen or undermine their confidence. Guidance offered with patience and love helps children learn from their mistakes while still believing in their own worth.

Father and Son

Audio
Image
Text

After a bitter argument with his father, a young man left home. Hurt and angry, his father refused even to hear his son’s name mentioned.

Every night, his mother went into his room, sat on the edge of the bed, and held his pillow close.

“I miss him,” she would say. “Please, let’s find him and bring him home.”

His father stayed silent and refused to discuss it.

Days became weeks, and weeks became months.

One day, unable to bear his wife’s tears any longer, the father took a folded piece of paper from his pocket.

“Go to this address. You’ll find him there. But don’t tell him I gave it to you.”

He added a few more words. She listened carefully, though her thoughts were already with her son.

The address led to a repair workshop. Her son was busy in his work clothes. For a while, she stood in the doorway watching him. Then she called his name, her voice trembling.

He looked up. For a moment, he could not believe his eyes. Then he hurried over and embraced her.

He asked after everyone, leaving his father until last.

“How is he? Is he still angry with me?”

His mother looked into his face.

“Come home, my dear. We miss you so much.”

His expression hardened.

“No, Mom. I don’t want to go back to someone who hasn’t looked for me even once. He’s probably happier without me.”

She handed him the sweets she had brought. Then she said quietly:

“I won’t force you. But there’s something your father asked me to tell you. He said to be careful of the man you’ve become friends with this past month. And he hopes you’ll make peace with your old friend.”

The young man froze.

“Dad… how does he know about that?”

His mother did not answer. She hugged him once more and left.

As soon as she got home, she asked her husband:

“If you knew where he was, why didn’t you tell me? How long have you known?”

His eyes filled with tears.

“Since the day he left. Sometimes I passed the workshop at lunchtime to see whether he was eating, whether he was all right. Some evenings, I went just to catch a glimpse of him before coming home.”

“Then why didn’t you go over and talk to him?”

He lowered his head.

“My pride wouldn’t let me. I thought he should make the first move… But every day, I hoped he would come home. He’s my son.”

Just then, there was a knock at the door.

The mother opened it and found her son standing outside. He came in and stopped in front of his father. For a few moments, neither spoke.

Then his father stepped towards him.

“I should have come and talked to you, son.”

Tears came to the young man’s eyes.

“And the things I said… I’m sorry, Dad.”

They embraced, and his mother joined them.

The hurt between them had not vanished in an instant. But now they were together, ready to begin talking it through.

Moral: Love that is never expressed in words or actions can feel like absence. What matters most is not who makes the first move towards reconciliation, but that someone makes it before more time is lost.

A father's advice

Audio
Image
Text

My dear son,

Let good judgment guide you through life. Be thoughtful in your dealings with others, measured in your words, and fair in your actions. Wisdom means looking beyond your own advantage and considering the rights of others.

Value your life. Its worth lies not simply in its length, but in how you live it. Make room each day for learning, love, and kindness.

As fruit sweetens with ripening, we mature by learning from what we experience. Listen to those with a wealth of life experience. But remember: wisdom comes not merely from growing older, but from reflecting on what life has taught us.

A thoughtless act in youth can trouble the heart for years. When making a decision, consider not only what you want today, but what will let you live with a clear conscience tomorrow. If you make a mistake, own it, put it right where you can, and learn from it.

The good you sow when you are young may bear fruit in later years. Honesty, diligence, and respect for others can sustain you throughout your life.

Strength is a gift, but it should serve judgment and conscience. Use it to protect and build, not to crush others.

Remember that none of us is here forever. Cherish the life God has given you. Let the thought of mortality deepen your appreciation of today and of the people beside you, rather than turn you away from life.

In my own life, I have tried to keep two simple habits: eating in moderation and staying active. I offer them not as a cure for every ailment, but as part of caring for myself. Pay attention to your body’s needs, and do not neglect them.

Notice, too, how people approach learning.

Some think carefully, ask questions, and consider the consequences of their actions.

Some have not yet found their way, but are willing to listen and learn. Good counsel can help them find direction.

Others neither recognize what they do not know nor listen to anyone else. Believing they know everything, they close the door to learning.

Strive to be thoughtful. Never be ashamed to ask when you do not know. Listen to advice and weigh it carefully. Above all, guard against the belief that you have nothing left to learn.

This is my wish for you: a clear mind, a peaceful conscience, and a heart open to learning.

Moral: Wisdom is not knowing everything, but recognizing what you do not know and being willing to learn. Let judgment guide you, conscience keep you true, and strength serve them both.

Stay calm, Arif

Audio
Image
Text

A grandfather was shopping with his grandson at the supermarket. As they walked down the aisles, putting groceries into their basket, the boy demanded everything he saw.

“Get me this! I want that too!”

Whenever his grandfather said no, the boy shouted even louder. Each time, the old man said quietly:

“Stay calm, Arif. We’re nearly finished. We’ll be home soon.”

Eventually, the boy grabbed a chocolate egg from a shelf, threw it on the floor, and stamped on it.

His grandfather took a deep breath.

“Stay calm, Arif. Just a little longer, and we’ll be going home.”

Then he turned to a nearby member of staff.

“I’m very sorry. I’ll pay for that too.”

After paying at the checkout, they left the store. A woman who had witnessed the whole scene followed them outside and approached the grandfather.

“I was so impressed by your patience! No matter how much Arif misbehaved, you never once raised your voice.”

The grandfather thanked her and smiled.

“The boy’s name is Rafiq. I’m Arif.”

Moral: Real strength is keeping your composure when things get difficult. Sometimes the first person who needs to hear “stay calm” is you.

Text

A father and his little daughter were crossing a bridge. Glancing at the river below, he grew a little anxious.

“Sweetheart, hold my hand tightly so you don’t fall.”

The girl looked up at him.

“No, Daddy. You hold mine.”

“What’s the difference?” he asked, puzzled.

“There’s a big difference! If I’m holding your hand, I might let go when I get scared. But if you’re holding mine, I know you won’t let go, no matter what.”

Moral: Trust means knowing that someone will hold on when you are afraid. Don’t wait for those you love to ask for help. Be there when they need you, and reach for their hand.

Mother and Son

Audio
Image
Text

The man set the suitcases against the wall, straightened up, and took a deep breath.

“Mom, this really is a lovely place.”

“Of course it is, son,” the elderly woman replied.

For a while, they stood in silence, looking around the room.

She opened her small suitcase and took out her nightdress, slippers, a few towels, and a framed photograph. She placed the photograph on the bedside table.

It showed a boy in school uniform, standing stiffly at attention. His eyes were full of tears, his lips drawn into a trembling pout.

The man went to the window and pulled back the curtain. Pale daylight entered the room. It was freezing outside. He rested his hands on the radiator and watched mud-spattered cars passing along the street.

“Will this wardrobe be big enough for you, Mom?” he asked without turning around.

She did not hear him. Sitting on the bed, leaning slightly to one side, she was gazing at the frightened child in the worn frame.

The photograph had been taken on his first day of school.

The memory returned as vividly as if it were yesterday. The evening before, she had spent hours telling him what a wonderful place school was. Yet the next morning, she had barely managed to get him into the classroom. She had stayed at school all day, going to him at every break.

“I’m here, son. I’m waiting for you.”

But each time, he had gripped her hand more tightly and cried:

“Don’t leave me, Mom. I’m so scared.”

The sun was setting. The man took his hands off the radiator and rested them against the misted window.

“Open the other suitcase too, Mom. Let’s put your things away.”

“Don’t let me keep you, son. I can do it myself. There isn’t much, anyway.”

She reached out with trembling hands and took his hand. For a moment, she seemed about to say something. Then she changed her mind. She swallowed.

“The children will be wondering where you are.”

The door creaked shut.

The elderly woman was left alone in the room with its peeling paint and the frightened little boy in the photograph. She sat on the edge of the bed for a while, then put on her slippers and went to the window.

Her son was getting into his car.

She was frightened. The day before, she had listened for hours to how lovely this place was. But she was still frightened.

His handprint remained on the glass. Gently, she rested her cheek against it. Her eyes filled with tears, and her lips began to tremble.

“Don’t leave me, son. I’m so scared,” she whispered.

Her words faded against the misted glass. Outside, her son sat in his car and waved.

That night, the other residents on her floor heard her sobbing. Such sounds were not unfamiliar in the corridors of the care home.

Moral: Growing older does not lessen our need for love and closeness. Caring for a parent means more than providing comfort and meeting daily needs; it also means helping them feel remembered and less alone.

Raising a Child

Audio
Image
Text

A boy often defied his parents. Whatever they told him to do, he did the opposite. Things were not going well at school either: he showed little interest in lessons and left his homework unfinished.

His mother hit him in anger, while his father shut him in his room as punishment. Neither approach made things better.

One day, as his mother was taking him to school, they came across a man performing with a bear. A crowd had gathered around them. The man played a tambourine, and the bear, with a ring through its nose, jumped and danced in response to his movements.

The woman stopped and thought, “I can’t manage one child, yet this man can make even a bear obey!”

She approached him.

“My son won’t listen to me. How did you train this animal?”

The man smiled.

“There’s a way to do everything. I have my own methods.”

“Could you help me too?”

The bear handler looked at the boy.

“Bring him to me for a week. I’ll teach him to do as he’s told.”

Delighted, the woman began taking her son to the man every day.

Within a few days, the boy changed. He no longer argued with his mother and quietly did what she asked. By the end of the week, she thought her problem was solved. She thanked the man and blessed him.

The neighbors were astonished. The once unruly boy now seemed quiet and obedient. His teacher was pleased too and congratulated his mother.

Before long, however, other problems emerged. The boy no longer rebelled openly, but he began secretly taking his classmates’ belongings, covering up what he had done, and lying.

His teacher called his mother.

“I’m concerned about some changes in your son’s behavior. He takes other children’s things and then denies it. We need to understand why.”

Distraught, the woman went looking for the bear handler. But he had already moved on.

Seeing the parents’ distress, an experienced neighbor said:

“I know a kind, wise man. Go and speak to him. Perhaps he can help you find a way forward.”

The mother took her son to see him and explained what had happened. The wise man said:

“Let me meet your son a few times over the coming week. I’d like to get to know him and understand what he has been through.”

After their second meeting, he asked to speak to the mother. He looked deeply concerned.

“Your son is afraid to admit when he has done something wrong. He believes that telling the truth will always lead to punishment. How have you been treating him?”

She described the punishments at home and then told him about the bear handler.

The wise man sighed.

“You wanted to change your child’s behavior, but you did not try to understand his feelings. Fear can silence a child for a while. But silence is not understanding, and obedience is not the same as learning right from wrong. A child who fears punishment may try to hide a mistake instead of putting it right.”

He paused, then continued:

“Raising a child means building trust, not breaking their will. Listen to your son, make time for him, and show him your love. Explain the rules clearly and follow them yourself. When he makes a mistake, help him understand its consequences and make amends without humiliating him.”

He concluded:

“I can offer guidance, but I cannot change your relationship at home for you. Change begins when your child feels safe, loved, and heard in your company.”

Moral: The aim of raising a child is to nurture understanding of right and wrong, rather than silence born of fear. Love, trust, clear boundaries, and a parent’s own example are the foundations of that process.

Puppies for Sale

Audio
Image
Text

A little boy saw a sign in a shop window that read “Puppies for Sale” and went inside.

“How much are the puppies?” he asked.

“Between thirty and fifty dollars,” the shopkeeper replied. “Each one has a different price.”

The boy took all the money from his pocket, placed it on the counter, and counted it carefully.

“I only have thirteen dollars and twenty-seven cents,” he said, disappointed. Then he asked hopefully:

“Could I at least look at them?”

The shopkeeper smiled and called to his wife in the back room. A moment later, she appeared with five adorable, plump puppies following her. One lagged behind, struggling to walk.

The boy could not take his eyes off it.

“What’s wrong with its leg?”

“The vet says it was born with a problem in one of its leg bones. It will always have difficulty walking.”

The boy immediately said:

“That’s the one I want!”

The shopkeeper looked surprised.

“I wouldn’t have expected you to choose that one. If you want it, you can have it for free.”

The boy’s expression changed. Looking straight at the shopkeeper, he said firmly:

“I don’t want you to give it to me for free. It’s worth just as much as the other puppies. I’ll give you thirteen dollars and twenty-seven cents now, then pay five dollars a week until I’ve paid the full thirty.”

The shopkeeper tried again to change his mind.

“But it won’t be able to run and play with you like the others.”

“It deserves a chance too. It’s just as valuable as the rest,” the boy replied.

Then he bent down and lifted his trouser leg. His leg, weakened by polio, was supported by metal braces.

He looked at the shopkeeper and said quietly:

“I can’t run and play either. This puppy and I both need a friend who understands us and gives us support.”

Moral: A living being’s worth is not measured by what it can or cannot do. True friendship means understanding and supporting one another, rather than turning away because of differences.

The Rotten Seed

Audio
Image
Text

After his wife died, a father refused to care for his young son, who was often ill. He dismissed the boy as a “rotten seed” and eventually placed him in a children’s home.

Years passed.

One day, the father saw his grown son outside a school. He could hardly believe his eyes. Before him stood a strong, healthy young man with bright, lively eyes.

Looking at him with regret, he exclaimed:

“I never thought you would recover and grow so strong. Look at you now! How did this happen? How did you overcome all those illnesses?”

The young man’s eyes filled with tears. He looked at the father who had once rejected him, then pointed to an elderly woman approaching them with a smile.

“After you said, ‘A rotten seed will never sprout,’ and left me at the children’s home, this woman adopted me. She said, ‘A seed watered with love deserves a chance to grow.’ She never withheld her love or care from me.”

Moral: A child’s worth is not measured by their health. Cruel words can crush hope, while love and steady care can help a child discover their strength. No child is a “rotten seed.”

Spinach

Audio
Image
Text

We lived in a remote village without even a proper road. My mother seemed to cook nothing but spinach. In my childish mind, I wondered whether my father’s death had left her unable to think clearly.

“Eat plenty. It will make you strong,” she would say.

Morning, noon, and night, there was spinach and cornbread on our table. I grew so tired of spinach that I could hardly bear to look at it.

One day, I told her:

“When I grow up, get a job, and have a family, I’ll never buy spinach. Nobody will cook it in my house!”

My mother smiled. She said she could hardly wait for that day and promised that, however old she was, she would dance at my wedding.

Eventually, I left the village to continue my secondary education in town. I scraped by on a small scholarship. When I came home once a month, I would not touch the spinach my mother served. I dreamed of returning to the dormitory and eating something different, something tastier.

I kept studying. With a government student loan, I completed university, then a master’s degree and a doctorate. By then, my mother had grown old, and her back was bent.

My fiancée and I returned to the village for our wedding. That day, my mother cried—and danced, just as she had promised all those years before. At the time, I did not understand why tears ran down her face as she danced.

The years passed. My mother died two years ago.

One day, in her old chest, I found a diary she had kept. On one page, she had written:

“All I had left was some spinach seed. I grew spinach and a little maize. We had next to nothing. I cooked the same meal over and over so my children would not go hungry. But I did not complain.

“‘Spinach is very good for you. That’s why I give it to you three times a day,’ I would tell them, and my heart would ache. It was all I could do for them. I love my children more than my own life.”

I learned to love spinach at fifty.

Now my wife often wonders why I buy it so frequently. She thinks I love the taste.

She does not know that whenever I smell it cooking, I remember everything my mother endured to raise us—and the way she danced at my wedding with tears streaming down her face.

Moral: Sometimes a meal we grew tired of as children concealed a mother’s poverty and quiet sacrifice. We should not have to wait years to recognize the love behind it.

Teeth falling out

Audio
Image
Text

A king dreamed that all his teeth had fallen out. In the morning, he summoned a dream interpreter.

“What does this dream mean?” he asked.

“Your Majesty, all your loved ones will die before you. You alone will remain.”

The king was shaken by these words. Then, in anger, he sent the interpreter away.

He summoned another interpreter and described the same dream.

The second interpreter replied:

“Your Majesty, the dream can be understood this way: you will live longer than anyone else in your family.”

The king’s face brightened. He rewarded the interpreter and sent him on his way.

The two interpretations meant the same thing. But one drew the king’s attention to loss, while the other focused on a long life.

Moral: The impact of words depends not only on their meaning, but also on how they are spoken. Wisdom includes telling the truth with consideration for the listener’s feelings.

The nail's lesson

Audio
Image
Text

In a village there lived a boy who was very quick-tempered and impatient. Even the slightest remark would make him angry, and when he lost his temper, he did not hesitate to hurt the person in front of him.

His father was deeply concerned about this side of his son's character. One day, he gave him a bag of nails and said:

‘My son, whenever you get angry with someone and say something hurtful, hammer one of these nails into the fence around the yard.’

At first, the boy thought this was pointless. But the next day he became angry and hammered a nail into the fence. Then he lost his temper again and hammered in another. As the days passed, the number of nails in the fence began to grow.

After a while, the boy began to pay attention to his behavior. Reaching for a nail every time he became angry made him think. At last, one day he went to his father and said:

‘Father, I will try not to lose my temper anymore.’

His father smiled and replied:

‘Very good. Now, for every day that you control yourself and hurt no one, pull one nail out of the fence.’

The boy did as he was told. Days passed, then months, and at last all the nails had been removed from the fence.

The boy called out joyfully to his father:

‘Father, look! I have pulled out all the nails.’

His father took him over to the fence and said quietly:

‘You have removed all the nails. But look: the marks they left are still there. The same thing happens when you get angry and lash out at someone with your words. You may apologize afterward, you may feel sorry, and that person may even forgive you. But a wound in the heart does not always heal without a scar.’

The boy looked at the holes in the fence for a long time.

From that day on, when he became angry, he learned first to remain silent and then to think.

Moral: A word spoken is like a nail: it is driven in during a moment of anger, but its mark may remain for a long time. That is why we must think before speaking and pause before causing hurt.

An hour with Dad

Image
Text

One evening, a little boy approached his father.

“Dad, can I ask you something?”

“Of course, son.”

“How much do you earn in an hour?”

His father frowned.

“Why do you want to know?”

“I’m just curious. Please tell me, Dad.”

At last, his father answered:

“I earn a hundred dollars an hour.”

The boy lowered his head and fell silent for a moment. Then he asked hesitantly:

“Could you lend me fifty dollars?”

His father grew angry.

“So that’s why you asked! You probably want another toy you don’t need. I don’t work all day for that. Go to your room and get to bed!”

Without a word, the boy went to his room and quietly closed the door.

His father paced for a while. As his anger faded, he realized he had never asked what his son wanted the money for. Perhaps it was something important to him.

Feeling ashamed, he went to the boy’s room and knocked softly.

“Are you asleep, son?”

“No, Dad. I’m awake.”

His father came in and sat beside him.

“I’m sorry I snapped at you earlier. I had a difficult day and took it out on you. Here are the fifty dollars you asked for.”

The boy thanked him happily. Then he pulled his savings from beneath his pillow and began to count them.

Seeing the money, his father grew angry again.

“If you already had money, why did you ask me for more?”

The boy gathered the notes and looked up at him.

“Because I didn’t have enough. Now I have exactly a hundred dollars. Dad, can I buy an hour of your time? Please come home early tomorrow so we can have dinner together.”

His father’s eyes filled with tears. He held his son close and apologized once more.

Moral: Caring for your family means more than earning money. Children need your love, your attention, and time with you—things no gift can replace.

Waiting for the teeth

Text

On a cold winter evening, an elderly couple walked into McDonald’s and sat down. The husband went to the counter and ordered a hamburger, a large portion of fries, and a large Coke.

Back at the table, he cut the hamburger in half and placed one half in front of his wife. He counted out the fries, dividing them equally. Then he took a sip of Coke and passed it to her.

People nearby watched sympathetically. “They must be short of money,” they thought. “They’re sharing a meal for one.”

The husband began eating with a healthy appetite. His wife left her food untouched, watching him and occasionally taking a sip of Coke.

A young employee noticed and approached their table.

“Excuse me, sir. I couldn’t help noticing that your wife hasn’t touched her food. Would she prefer something else? I’d be happy to bring it.”

The man smiled.

“Thank you, son, but we’re quite all right. We’ve been married for sixty years. We share everything.”

Still puzzled, the employee turned to the wife.

“Then what are you waiting for, ma’am?”

She nodded toward her husband and replied calmly:

“The teeth, dear. I’m waiting for the teeth!”

Moral: Everyone has their own way of living, and every family has its own habits. What seems unusual to us may be an ordinary part of someone else’s daily life.
